Gate ladder, all green: **L0** knob-off bit-identical to the C oracle on every build; **L1**
`MEVPLAG=1` byte-identical (with the null-rung announce proving it resolved) and `MEVPNOEPS`
**exactly** zero; **L2** selfcheck intra-step drift **1e-15 … 2.5e-15 relative**, flat across
20 steps × 120 subcycles; **L4** CUDA-vs-Serial PASS both knob states; **L5** options ×3 — TKE
and zstar **bit-identical** with all M9 knobs set, i.e. the knobs provably cannot reach outside
the `whichEVP==1` branch.

**The reformulation is exact in practice.** Within an ocean step the masks are fixed and
`R ≡ ∇·σ` holds to machine precision with no growth over the run — direct evidence that
Danilov's algebra survives the discretisation, not merely that the ice looks plausible. The
cross-step mask-flip non-equivalence is bounded by measurement at **2.1e-11 relative**, firing
only on steps where `ice_el` membership actually changes.

⭐ **Better framing than "grows with rank count": the lever pays in proportion to the halo cost
already being paid, which is largest AT AND PAST THE STRONG-SCALING KNEE.** Classic baselines
(s/step) by node count:

| classic | 1 N | 2 N | 4 N | 8 N | 16 N |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|

| farc GPU | — | 0.1272 | **0.1178** | 0.1179 | 0.1169 |

CORE2 is *past* its knee at 2 nodes and then **degrades** — more GPUs, slower model — and that
is exactly where lag8 recovers the most (−25.9 % at 16 N). farc is **flat from 4 nodes**
(0.1178 / 0.1179 / 0.1169), and its lag8 gain peaks at 8 N and dips at 16 N accordingly. The
np128 (32-node) point was cancelled as pointless once the flatness was established.

**H2 confirmed on both backends and both meshes.** **The CPU/GPU asymmetry remains the
strongest structural result** — the same one-line lever is worth −13…−26 % of a GPU step and
−1…−3.4 % of a CPU step, because the ice phase is a far larger share of a GPU step.

⚠️ **UNVERIFIED, flagged for the next session:** `docs/SCALING_FARC.md` (2026-05-28) records
farc GPU at **2.1225 s/step on 2 nodes**; this campaign measures **0.1272** — a factor of ~17.
Plausible contributors, none confirmed: the M7 campaign (~8×), monthly IO disabled here via
`io.config.m9_timing`, the per-step diagnostic `Gatherv` suppressed via `PRINT_EVERY=999`, and
the launch-bound finding (a 5× larger mesh costing only ~2× more time when the GPU is mostly
idle is consistent with §4). **Do not quote these farc absolutes until that is decomposed.**
The **ratios** are unaffected — every leg runs in the same allocation, same binary, same config.

⚠️ At CPU np512 the reference leg's own rep spread was 3.8 %, so the −2.40 % whole-step figure
is near its own noise; the `icedyn` numbers there (1.7 → 1.3 ms) are the trustworthy ones.

**The K sweep saturates at K ≈ 8–12** on both backends (GPU np32: −71 % at K=8, −73 % at K=12,
−80 % at K=120; CPU np256: −17.9 % at K=8 and unchanged through K=60). So there is no reason to
go beyond K≈8–12, which is also where the approximation is mildest.

## 3. Cell ③ (the divergence form) — neutral on CPU, a small cost on GPU

| | icedyn | step |
|---|---|---|
| CPU np1 (largest per-rank) | −0.65 % | −0.20 % |
| CPU np8 | −1.4 % | −0.30 % |
| CPU np64 / np256 / np512 | 0 % | +0.07 / −0.29 / −0.34 % |
| GPU np8 | **+13 %** | +1.6 % |
| GPU ng5_w3d np16 / np64 | +7.4 % / +6.6 % | +0.02 % / +0.49 % |

**H1 is falsified.** Win A was predicted to grow with per-rank workload as σ stops being
cache-resident. It does not appear at any per-rank size measured, on either backend, and the
mechanism measurement says why (§4). `FESOM_SPEED_MEVPNOEPS` — which removes three element
stores per element per subcycle — measures **exactly 0.00 %** in both forms at every point,
independently confirming there was no memory-traffic saving available to collect.

The GPU cost is real but not isolated: it is **not** kernel time (§4), and the ice-element-mask
ablation (`MEVPDIV_NOMASK`) changed it by ~1 % of itself (9.4 vs 9.5 ms), so the node kernel is
not the cause either. **I do not have an isolated mechanism for the residual and am not
offering one.**

## 4. Mechanism, measured

nsys, validated per the project's meta-lesson before use: the traced classic→div ratio
reproduces the untraced A/B (+1.76 % vs +1.63 %), while absolute traced step times are 48 %
inflated and were therefore not used.

- Total GPU kernel-busy is **identical** between forms: 21.8 vs 21.7 ms/step.
- The mEVP kernels are marginally **cheaper** in the divergence form: 1.24 vs 1.27 ms/step.
- Of the **8.2 ms** `icedyn` phase at CORE2 np8, only **~1.25 ms is kernel execution**, spread
  over ~480 launches/step. Launch count differs by exactly 3 (the mask kernels): 487 → 490.

**The mEVP subcycle on GPU at this size is launch/exchange-bound, not compute-bound.** That is
why removing whole exchanges (cell ⑤) pays enormously while removing element bytes (cell ③,
`MEVPNOEPS`) pays nothing. It also predicts the regime where Win A could matter: many fewer,
much larger kernels — i.e. far larger per-rank workloads than anything reachable here.

## 5. Accuracy of cell ⑤ — NOT yet established, and it gates adoption

300-step CORE2 np8, max|Δ| vs classic:

| K | 2 | 4 | 8 | 12 | 30 | 120 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `a_ice` | 1.3e-1 | 3.2e-2 | 5.7e-2 | 3.5e-2 | 5.4e-2 | 8.9e-2 |
| `uice` | 9.5e-2 | 9.5e-2 | 9.6e-2 | 1.9e-1 | 2.1e-1 | 1.8e-1 |

**There is no monotone trend with K**, and every large value is carried by 1–5 points out of
126 858. That is the signature of chaotic divergence at the ice edge after 6.25 simulated days,
not of a systematic K-dependent bias — a K=2 run (the mildest possible approximation) shows a
larger `a_ice` outlier than K=30.

**Consequence: `max|Δ|` at 300 steps cannot measure this approximation's cost.** The honest
metrics are RMS/pattern statistics and a climate leg (L6), which the plan defers. Until those
run, cell ⑤ is a **proven speed result with an unquantified accuracy cost**, and must not be
recommended for production on the strength of §2 alone.

## 5b. farc at thousands of ranks — the configuration Koldunov et al. (2019) diagnosed

`/pool/data/AWICM/FESOM2/MESHES_FESOM2.1/farc` (638 k nodes, 47 levels), dt=900, 100 steps,
CPU, rank ladder 512 → 2304 (that mesh copy's partition ceiling). **This is the same mesh as
GMD 12, 3991 (2019) §Fig. 7**, which reports sea ice growing from **16 % of runtime at 144
cores to 33 % at 6912**, blames one halo exchange per EVP subcycle (~100/step) with too little
arithmetic to amortise the MPI, plus cores without ice idling in `MPI_Wait`, and proposes —
untested at the time — **"wider halo regions to reduce communication frequency"**. Cell ④ is
that proposal implemented exactly; cell ⑤ is its cheap approximation.

**Ice share of the step** (ice + icedyn + iceadv, busy **and** wait, matching the paper's
definition — quoting `icedyn` busy alone undercounts it by ~3×):

| ranks | 512 | 1024 | 1536 | 2048 | 2304 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| ice share | 11.4 % | 11.4 % | 12.6 % | 12.9 % | **13.2 %** |
| lag8 → model step | −1.1 % | −1.8 % | −1.2 % | −1.6 % | **−3.4 %** |
| lag8 → icedyn busy | −11.0 % | −8.6 % | −10.2 % | −12.8 % | −14.3 % |

The share **grows with core count, in the same direction as the paper**, but is lower in
absolute terms (13 % at 2304 vs the paper's ~25–30 % interpolated there) and rises more slowly.
Two candidate reasons, neither tested: the port has had a long comms-optimisation campaign
(CGPIPE, fused halos, device-resident state) that the 2019 FESOM2.0 had not; and these runs use
mEVP where the paper used standard EVP.

### ⭐ The mechanism is confirmed, and it is `MPI_Wait`, not kernel time

np2304, classic → lag8, from the same instrumented run:

| phase | busy | **wait** | mpi/step |
|---|---|---|---|
| `icedyn` classic | 3.5 ms | **4.8 ms** | 240 |
| `icedyn` lag8 | 3.0 ms (−14 %) | **1.9 ms (−60 %)** | 30 |
| `ocean`, `cg` | unchanged | unchanged | unchanged |

Total ice cost 8.3 → 4.9 ms (**−41 %**); whole-model MPI ops/step 987 → 777 (the −210 is
exactly the saved ice exchanges). **The lever pays by removing idle, not by computing faster** —
precisely the mechanism the 2019 paper identifies. The exchange count drops exactly 8× (240 →
30), which is also the knob-fired proof.

Note what this implies for cell ③: the divergence form is ~0 on farc at every rank count
(−1.4 % … +1.0 % on `icedyn`), because it changes kernel work, and kernel work is not what costs.

### Where the ceiling is

At np2304 `cg` (the SSH solver) carries **21.7 ms of the 43.7 ms total wait** — five times the
ice phase's wait after lagging. So on this mesh, past ~2000 ranks the dominant imbalance is the
SSH solve, not the ice. That bounds what any ice lever can deliver here and matches the paper's
observation that "SSH and sea-ice calculations become more expensive than the 3-D part".

## 6. Limitations

1. **~~The production pain was not reproduced~~ — ADDRESSED (§5b).** farc at 512–2304 ranks now
   covers it: ice share 11.4 % → 13.2 %, lag8 worth −3.4 % of the step at 2304, mechanism
   confirmed as `MPI_Wait` removal. Two gaps remain: (a) the ladder stops at **2304** because
   `MESHES_FESOM2.1/farc` has no larger partition — the 2019 paper's 6912 partitioning lives in
   `/pool/…/MESHES/fArc_sorted`, which **this port currently cannot run** (CG non-convergence
   immediately, at dt 900/300/180, with and without wsplit — not a cold-start CFL blowup, which
   develops over tens of steps); (b) the measured ice share is ~half the paper's at comparable
   scale, unexplained.
   ⚠️ Two hours were lost to running `fArc_sorted` believing it was the project's farc: it has
   the same 638 387 nodes, so a node-count check passes, but a different partition set. The
   project's mesh root is `MESHES_FESOM2.1/` — as `jobs/*` already state for dars and ng5.
   **Check the repo's own mesh-root convention before picking a directory by name.**
2. Cells ②/④ are not built, so the study's central ②-vs-④ comparison — whether the divergence
   form's single fused message beats the classic form's two — is **unanswered**.
3. CORE2 np512's whole-step figures sit near their own rep spread (§2).
4. `ng5_w3d` CPU np64 initially OOM'd at 64 ranks on one node; rerun across 4 nodes. Timings
   for that mesh therefore come from a different ranks-per-node packing than the CORE2 runs and
   are not directly comparable across meshes (they are valid within a mesh, which is all the
   A/B needs).
