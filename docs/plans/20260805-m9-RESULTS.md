## 4. Mechanism, measured

nsys, validated per the project's meta-lesson before use: the traced classic→div ratio
reproduces the untraced A/B (+1.76 % vs +1.63 %), while absolute traced step times are 48 %
inflated and were therefore not used.

- Total GPU kernel-busy is **identical** between forms: 21.8 vs 21.7 ms/step.
- The mEVP kernels are marginally **cheaper** in the divergence form: 1.24 vs 1.27 ms/step.
- Of the **8.2 ms** `icedyn` phase at CORE2 np8, only **~1.25 ms is kernel execution**, spread
  over ~480 launches/step. Launch count differs by exactly 3 (the mask kernels): 487 → 490.

**The mEVP subcycle on GPU at this size is launch/exchange-bound, not compute-bound.** That is
why removing whole exchanges (cell ⑤) pays enormously while removing element bytes (cell ③,
`MEVPNOEPS`) pays nothing. It also predicts the regime where Win A could matter: many fewer,
much larger kernels — i.e. far larger per-rank workloads than anything reachable here.

## 5. Accuracy of cell ⑤ — MEASURED (session 2). It passes, with a thinner margin than predicted

Pre-registration: `20260805-m9-PREREG-P1.md`, committed `c9a4d6a` **before** the first leg ran.
1-year CORE2 legs, dt 1800, 17 280 steps, year 1958, mEVP, private mesh.

### 5.0 Why the session-1 numbers could not answer this

300-step `max|Δ|` vs classic showed **no monotone trend with K**, every large value carried by
1–5 points of 126 858 — chaotic ice-edge divergence, not a K-dependent bias (K=2 showed a
larger `a_ice` outlier than K=30). That metric cannot measure this approximation, so the
pre-registration fixed **pattern statistics** as the metrics of record and explicitly demoted
`max|Δ|` to "reported, not a criterion". It is still reported below and still ignored.

### 5.1 The controls, which decide which arm may be read

| control | result |
|---|---|
| **CPU** classic vs classic_rep (same everything) | **all 17 monthly files BIT-IDENTICAL** over a full year |
| **GPU** classic vs classic_rep (same everything) | `uice` corr **0.9723**, `vice` **0.9589**, sst RMS 2.1e-3 |

The Serial backend at fixed rank count is deterministic, so the CPU arm's zero is exactly zero
and **every CPU difference below is the approximation and nothing else**. CUDA is not
deterministic (D22), and a year of compounding puts its replicate's `uice` departure at
2.8e-2 — **the same order as the lever's own**. So, exactly as control 2 pre-registered,
**the GPU arm cannot resolve the approximation and the CPU arm carries the verdict.** That is
an anticipated outcome, not a failure; the GPU arm still confirms every field clears the floor.

## 5b. farc at thousands of ranks — the configuration Koldunov et al. (2019) diagnosed

`/pool/data/AWICM/FESOM2/MESHES_FESOM2.1/farc` (638 k nodes, 47 levels), dt=900, 100 steps,
CPU, rank ladder 512 → 2304 (that mesh copy's partition ceiling). **This is the same mesh as
GMD 12, 3991 (2019) §Fig. 7**, which reports sea ice growing from **16 % of runtime at 144
cores to 33 % at 6912**, blames one halo exchange per EVP subcycle (~100/step) with too little
arithmetic to amortise the MPI, plus cores without ice idling in `MPI_Wait`, and proposes —
untested at the time — **"wider halo regions to reduce communication frequency"**. Cell ④ is
that proposal implemented exactly; cell ⑤ is its cheap approximation.

**Ice share of the step** (ice + icedyn + iceadv, busy **and** wait, matching the paper's
definition — quoting `icedyn` busy alone undercounts it by ~3×):

| ranks | 512 | 1024 | 1536 | 2048 | 2304 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| ice share | 11.4 % | 11.4 % | 12.6 % | 12.9 % | **13.2 %** |
| lag8 → model step | −1.1 % | −1.8 % | −1.2 % | −1.6 % | **−3.4 %** |
| lag8 → icedyn busy | −11.0 % | −8.6 % | −10.2 % | −12.8 % | −14.3 % |

The share **grows with core count, in the same direction as the paper**, but is lower in
absolute terms (13 % at 2304 vs the paper's ~25–30 % interpolated there) and rises more slowly.
Two candidate reasons, neither tested: the port has had a long comms-optimisation campaign
(CGPIPE, fused halos, device-resident state) that the 2019 FESOM2.0 had not; and these runs use
mEVP where the paper used standard EVP.

### ⭐ The mechanism is confirmed, and it is `MPI_Wait`, not kernel time

np2304, classic → lag8, from the same instrumented run:

| phase | busy | **wait** | mpi/step |
|---|---|---|---|
| `icedyn` classic | 3.5 ms | **4.8 ms** | 240 |
| `icedyn` lag8 | 3.0 ms (−14 %) | **1.9 ms (−60 %)** | 30 |
| `ocean`, `cg` | unchanged | unchanged | unchanged |

Total ice cost 8.3 → 4.9 ms (**−41 %**); whole-model MPI ops/step 987 → 777 (the −210 is
exactly the saved ice exchanges). **The lever pays by removing idle, not by computing faster** —
precisely the mechanism the 2019 paper identifies. The exchange count drops exactly 8× (240 →
30), which is also the knob-fired proof.

Note what this implies for cell ③: the divergence form is ~0 on farc at every rank count
(−1.4 % … +1.0 % on `icedyn`), because it changes kernel work, and kernel work is not what costs.

### Where the ceiling is

At np2304 `cg` (the SSH solver) carries **21.7 ms of the 43.7 ms total wait** — five times the
ice phase's wait after lagging. So on this mesh, past ~2000 ranks the dominant imbalance is the
SSH solve, not the ice. That bounds what any ice lever can deliver here and matches the paper's
observation that "SSH and sea-ice calculations become more expensive than the 3-D part".

## 8. Cells ②/④ — the EXACT wide halo, BUILT and byte-certified (session 2)

This is the 2019 paper's own proposal, implemented: *"wider halo regions to reduce
communication frequency"*. Cell ⑤ is its cheap approximation and needs the accuracy argument of
§5; **cells ②/④ are exact and need none** — they reproduce the every-subcycle trajectory
bit-for-bit.

| cell | carried state | window message | ships |
|---|---|---|---|
| ② classic form | σ11/σ12/σ22 on ghost elements | node segment + element segment | **8·Ng** doubles |
| ④ divergence form | R_u/R_v at ghost nodes | **one** node segment | **4·Ng** doubles |

### Gate ladder — all green (Serial FORCE_SERIAL, np8 CORE2, 20 steps, mEVP)

| rung | result | job |
|---|---|---|
| L0 knob-off vs the C oracle | bit-identical | 26711787 + every rung below |
| L1 `EVPWIDE=1` null rung | bit-identical | 26711788 |
| **cell ② K=4 / K=8 vs classic** | **BIT-IDENTICAL** | 26712292 / 26711922 |
| **cell ④ K=4 / K=8 vs the divergence form** | **BIT-IDENTICAL** | 26712290 / 26712291 |
| L4 CUDA fidelity, both cells | PASS, worst 1.5e-3 (the documented floor) | 26712516 / 26712517 |
| L5 options ×3 | TKE + zstar **bit-identical** ⇒ knobs inert outside `whichEVP==1` | 26712518 |

Not vacuous: the build reports **934 updatable ghost slots, 1913 ghost elements, subcycle
exchanges/step 120 → 30+1**. Cell ④'s reference is the divergence form, not knob-off — the
divergence form is solver-class *by design* (that is cell ③), so comparing ④ to the default
would fail for a reason unrelated to the wide halo and hide the only thing the rung asks.

### Two defects the byte gate caught and inspection did not

Cell ② was bit-identical from the first run while ④ failed at 3.0e-4 in `a_ice`; that
asymmetry — same rings, same ship, same maps, same solve tail — is what localised both.

1. **The ghost gather was timed wrong, and raced.** ④ stores no element stress, so its gather
   must recompute it from `u_aux` — but it sat inside the ghost *node* kernel, which runs after
   the owned node solve has already overwritten `u_aux` at every owned node. It recomputed from
   post-solve velocities where the owner had assembled from pre-solve ones. The same kernel also
   read `u_aux` at other ghost slots while writing `u_aux` at ghost slots: a real read-write
   race, benign-looking on Serial and nondeterministic on CUDA. Fixed by staging the gather in
   its own pass between the owned element kernel and any node update. **Cell ② is immune by
   construction** — its stress is computed and *stored* by the element pass, so reading it later
   is safe. → **L106**.
2. **The ghost node mask was a scatter where it had to be a gather.** It looped ghost *elements*
   and set `nod_el` at their three vertices — and a ghost element's vertices include *owned*
   nodes, so it reached back into owned state. Now each ghost slot asks the owner's question
   with the owner's data (does any element of my gather row carry ice?), which is ghost-only by
   construction. This one did not change the failing bytes — defect 1 dominated — but it was
   wrong on its own terms.

### 8.1 What they cost — and the honest headline is that EXACTNESS IS NOT FREE

GPU CORE2, dt 1800, 300 steps, CLEAN legs, all six cells in the same allocation (26712790 /
26712791 / 26712926). Change in **model step** vs classic:

| | 2 N (np8) | 8 N (np32) | 16 N (np64) |
|---|---|---|---|
| classic (s/step) | 0.0615 | 0.0763 | 0.0885 |
| **⑤ lag8** (approximate) | **−15.3 %** | **−20.8 %** | **−25.5 %** |
| ② wide K=8 (exact) | +0.3 % | −7.7 % | −12.5 % |
| ④ wide K=8 (exact) | +2.0 % | −8.4 % | **−15.3 %** |
| ② wide K=4 | +1.0 % | −5.1 % | — |
| ④ wide K=4 | +3.1 % | −5.5 % | — |

**The exact wide halo delivers roughly half to 60 % of what the one-line approximation does,
and at 2 nodes it delivers nothing at all.** That is not a disappointment to be explained away —
it follows directly from §4's measured mechanism. The mEVP subcycle is **launch/exchange-bound**,
and the two cells do opposite things to the launch count: cell ⑤ removes exchanges and adds
*nothing*, while cells ②/④ remove the same exchanges but add two to three ghost kernel launches
per subcycle plus the ghost arithmetic. At small rank counts the added launches cancel the
removed messages exactly; the exchange term only wins as rank count grows.

Both exact cells' gains **grow monotonically with rank count** (② 0 → −7.7 → −12.5 %,
④ +2.0 → −8.4 → −15.3 %) and the gap to cell ⑤ narrows with scale (16 N: −15.3 % vs −25.5 %,
i.e. 60 % of it). Extrapolating that trend past 16 N is exactly the kind of guess this project
does not make — it wants the measurement.

### 8.2 H3 answered, and this is where Danilov's reformulation finally pays

The pre-registration's H3 said ② − ④ is dominated by the **2× byte ratio** (8·Ng vs 4·Ng).

| | 2 N | 8 N | 16 N |
|---|---|---|---|
| **④ − ②** (percentage points of the model step) | **−1.6 pp** (④ worse) | **+0.7 pp** | **+2.7 pp** (④ better) |

**The divergence form loses at small scale and wins at scale, and the crossing is the result.**
④ ships half the bytes but pays one extra gather pass; where the message dominates (16 N) the
byte saving wins by 2.7 pp, and where it does not (2 N) the extra pass costs 1.6 pp.

This matters for how the whole study reads. **H1 was falsified** — the reformulation buys
nothing per element (§3: `MEVPNOEPS` measures *exactly* 0.00 %, because the subcycle is not
compute-bound). So on its own the divergence form is a null result. Its value is entirely in
what it does to the *communication*: it makes everything carried a node quantity, so the wide
halo needs one segment of 4·Ng instead of two totalling 8·Ng. §8.1–8.2 is the first measurement
in this campaign where that pays, and it pays only in the regime the reformulation was aimed at.

### 8.3 What this means for the recommendation

For **speed alone, cell ⑤ at K=4 remains the option to use** — it is worth more than the exact
cells at every rank count measured, and §5 characterises its accuracy cost (inside mEVP's own
floor, 1.2–1.5× at K=4). Cells ②/④ are the right choice for anyone who wants the exchange
reduction with **no accuracy argument whatsoever**, and their case strengthens with rank count.

| mesh | nodes | ranks | nodes/rank | classic | ⑤ lag8 | ② K=8 | ④ K=8 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| farc | 4 | 16 | 39 899 | 0.1168 | −14.2 % | −1.97 | −1.54 |
| farc | 8 | 32 | 19 950 | 0.1172 | −17.9 % | −4.61 | **−6.48** |
| farc | 16 | 64 | 9 975 | 0.1169 | −14.9 % | −1.80 | −3.76 |
| dars | 4 | 16 | 197 521 | 0.2344 | −5.8 % | +0.43 | −0.68 |
| dars | 8 | 32 | 98 761 | 0.1727 | −15.8 % | −4.98 | −7.06 |
| dars | 16 | 64 | 49 380 | 0.1397 | −20.7 % | −6.59 | **−9.38** |
| NG5 | 4 | 16 | 462 680 | 0.6510 | −2.8 % | +0.00 | — |
| NG5 | 16 | 64 | 115 670 | 0.2458 | −9.7 % | +0.04 | −2.36 |

⭐ **The difference between farc and dars is the whole point.** farc GPU is already **flat** past
4 nodes (0.1168 / 0.1172 / 0.1169) — there is no strong-scaling loss left to recover, so the
wide halo peaks at 8 nodes (−6.5 %) and falls back at 16 (−3.8 %). dars is **still scaling**
(0.2344 → 0.1727 → 0.1397), and there the wide halo keeps growing monotonically to **−9.4 % at
16 nodes**, the best exact-cell result of the campaign. NG5 at 462 k nodes/rank is the opposite
extreme: the per-rank subdomain is so large that no halo lever does anything (⑤ itself only
−2.8 %).

**So the exact cells' regime is: GPU, on a mesh that has not yet hit its strong-scaling knee.**

### 9.3 K on GPU — the full sweep, K = 2 / 4 / 8

| GPU point | ⑤ lag2 | ⑤ lag4 | ⑤ lag8 | ② K=2 | ④ K=2 | ④ K=4 | ④ K=8 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| farc 4 N | −8.6 % | −12.3 % | −14.2 % | +5.74 | +1.80 | +0.77 | −1.54 |
| farc 8 N | −9.7 % | −15.0 % | −17.9 % | +1.11 | −3.15 | −4.86 | −6.48 |
| farc 16 N | −8.8 % | −13.0 % | −14.9 % | +3.66 | −0.09 | −2.89 | −3.76 |
| dars 8 N | −9.3 % | −14.3 % | −15.8 % | +4.88 | −2.26 | −5.63 | −7.06 |

🔴 **K=2 is the WORST choice for the exact cells and the direction is opposite to cell ⑤'s.**
Both exact cells improve monotonically with K (K=2 → 4 → 8), because the exchange saving grows
faster than the ring zone does; cell ⑤ meanwhile wants *small* K on accuracy grounds (§5.4).
So the two options do not share a recommended K: **cells ②/④ want K=8, cell ⑤ wants K=4.**

Note also that at K=2 the ② form is **uniformly bad** (+1.1 to +5.7 %) while ④ is near neutral —
at small K there is little message to save, so ②'s second (element σ) segment is pure overhead.

### 9.5 Mechanism — measured, and it settles the question

farc CPU np512, `icedyn` phase, matched instrumented legs (job 26713772):

| leg | busy | wait | mpi/step | model step |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| classic | 14.5 ms | 8.7 ms | 240 | — |
| ⑤ lag8 | 12.9 ms (−11 %) | 8.2 ms | **30** | −0.75 % |
| ④ wide K=2 | 16.6 ms (+14 %) | 9.6 ms | 61 | +1.79 % |
| ④ wide K=8 | **39.1 ms (+170 %)** | **25.3 ms (+190 %)** | **16** | **+19.3 %** |

🔴 **The exact wide halo removes MORE messages than the approximation — 16 per step against
30 — and still loses, because the ghost compute it buys them with is larger than everything it
saves.** Ghost work also *adds* wait (8.7 → 25.3 ms): the ring zone is a different size on every
rank, so duplicating it introduces a new load imbalance on top of the one it removes.

And the same measurement on the **winning** side, farc GPU 16 N (job 26713773), which is what
explains the whole CPU/GPU split:

| leg | busy | wait | mpi/step | model step |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| classic | 12.1 ms | 10.9 ms | 120 | — |
| ⑤ lag8 | **3.5 ms (−71 %)** | **1.4 ms (−87 %)** | 15 | **−15.1 %** |
| ② wide K=8 | 12.0 ms (−1 %) | 6.9 ms (−37 %) | 16 | −2.2 % |
| ④ wide K=8 | 13.3 ms (+10 %) | 4.2 ms (−61 %) | 16 | −3.9 % |

⭐ **This is the campaign's cleanest single result.** On GPU an mEVP exchange costs mostly
*launches and packing*, which the profiler charges to **busy**, not to wait — so cell ⑤ removing
105 of 120 exchanges removes 71 % of the ice phase's busy time outright. The exact cells remove
the same 105 exchanges but **substitute ghost kernels for them one-for-one**, so busy does not
fall at all (12.0) or rises (13.3). **The approximation removes work; the exact halo only
relocates it.** That single sentence covers both backends and every mesh in §9.1–9.2.

It also shows H3 directly in the phase split: ④ has *higher* busy than ② (13.3 vs 12.0 — the
extra gather pass) but *lower* wait (4.2 vs 6.9 — half the bytes on the wire), and the wait term
wins, which is exactly why ④ beats ② on GPU and loses to it on CPU.

### 9.6 The controlling variable

Not rank count, not mesh size: the **ghost/owned ratio** — K-ring updatable slots per owned node
per rank, which goes like K·perimeter/area:

| point | K=2 | K=4 | K=8 |
|---|---|---|---|
| dars GPU np16 (197 k nodes/rank) | — | 0.03 | 0.08 |
| farc GPU np16 (39.9 k) | — | 0.07 | 0.18 |
| farc GPU np32 (19.9 k) | — | 0.10 | 0.25 |
| farc CPU np512 (1247) | 0.19 | 0.68 | — |
| farc CPU np2048 (312) | 0.30 | **1.18** | — |

At farc CPU np2048 the K=4 ring zone is **larger than the rank's own subdomain**, and that row
is the +8.96 %. The ratio is what a future adoption rule should be written against.

### 9.7 ② vs ④ across three meshes — H3 confirmed, and the crossing is backend-shaped

④ minus ② in percentage points of the model step (negative = ④ better):

| | farc GPU 4 N | farc GPU 8 N | farc GPU 16 N | dars GPU 8 N | dars GPU 16 N | NG5 GPU 16 N | farc CPU (K=4) | dars CPU (K=4) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| at K=8 | +0.4 | **−1.9** | **−2.0** | **−2.1** | **−2.8** | **−2.4** | — | — |
| at K=2 | **−3.9** | **−4.3** | **−3.8** | **−7.1** | — | — | +0.3…+0.9 | −0.1 |
| at K=4 | — | — | — | — | — | **−4.4** | +2.1…+5.4 | +0.8 |

**On GPU ④ wins essentially everywhere, on all three meshes, and by more at small K** — where ②'s
second (element σ) segment is a fixed overhead against a small message saving. **On CPU ② wins**
(messages are cheap there, so ④'s extra gather pass is pure cost). That is H3's byte-vs-launch
trade, confirmed on three meshes and visible directly in the phase split (§9.5).

### 9.8 What this means

The exact wide halo is **certified correct and, on current hardware, not the option to adopt for
speed.** Cell ⑤ at K=4 beats it everywhere measured — by 8–13 pp on GPU and by 2–8 pp on CPU —
and §5 has now characterised ⑤'s accuracy cost as inside mEVP's own reproducibility floor. The
exact cells' remaining case is narrow and real: a GPU run on a mesh still strong-scaling, where
**−9.4 % (dars, 16 nodes, cell ④, K=8) is available with no accuracy argument whatsoever**.

### What is NOT done
- **D1's fused build of ② is not implemented.** ② currently ships its node and element segments
  as two messages per partner (the unfused form). The fused variant is what makes H3's
  message-count term zero by construction, and it is the remaining piece of the user's D1
  decision. Note ②'s measured deficit vs ④ is consistent with bytes *and* with the extra
  message; the fused build is what separates them.
