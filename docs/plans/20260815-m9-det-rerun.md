## 1. What the fix changes, in terms this track cares about

`extrap_nod3D` fills the PHC dummy nodes — every node the 1° climatology leaves without a value,
which in practice means the marginal seas — by Gauss-Seidel averaging run to local exhaustion on
each rank between halo exchanges. First fill wins. **The result is therefore a function of the
domain decomposition**: change the rank count and the Marmara, the Gibraltar sill and their like
come out holding different water. `FESOM_IC_EXTRAP=det` replaces that with a two-phase Jacobi fill
(ring fill, then relaxation of the filled nodes to quasi-harmonic) with gid-ordered sums and a
halo exchange per sweep, which gives the same initial condition on every partition.

For M9 this splits the campaign cleanly in two:

| exposure | which M9 results | why |
|---|---|---|
| **Exposed** | the strong-scaling figures (report figs 2 and 3) | rank count is the x-axis, so **every point ran a different partition and therefore a different ocean**. The headline — the divergence form's saving *grows* with node count — is a statement about the shape of that curve. |
| **Partly exposed** | the operating-point table (Sect. 5.3) and Figure 1 | each point holds its partition fixed, so both sides of every A/B shared one IC and the **ratios are protected**. The absolute ice cost per mesh, and the cross-mesh ordering claim that compares four points at four different partitions, are not. |
| **Not exposed** | byte rungs, the K sweeps, the nsys launch traces, the 60-day imprint gate | all compare legs at one partition, including their controls. The IC is common-mode and cancels. |

The imprint gate deserves a note rather than a re-run. Its question is whether the lean wide halo
leaves the decomposition in the ice field, measured as a boundary/interior ratio against two
identical runs at the same partition. That control cancels the IC exactly. But it is worth
recording that under the legacy fill **the initial condition itself carries a decomposition
imprint** — the gate could not have seen it, and it is an argument for det independent of anything
M9 measured.

## 3. fArc_sorted — the blocked experiment that stayed blocked

Report Limitation 1(a) says the fArc rank ladder stops at 2304 because the 2019 paper's 6912-way
partitioning lives in `/pool/…/MESHES/fArc_sorted`, which **this port cannot run**: CG fails to
converge immediately, at dt 900/300/180, with and without wsplit. That signature — a solve that
diverges at step 1 rather than a CFL failure developing over tens of steps — is close enough to
the M13 failure mode that it was the first thing to re-test.

**It is not the same bug.** Two same-allocation legacy-vs-det pairs, 50 steps at dt 900:

| job | partition | legacy | det |
|---|---|---|---|
| 26961292 | `fArc_sorted/dist_288` | CG diverges at step 1, rtol 3.6885e+01 | CG diverges at step 1, rtol **3.8718e+00** |
| 26961293 | `fArc_sorted/dist_1152` | CG diverges at step 1, rtol 7.4385e+01 | CG diverges at step 1, rtol **3.8718e+00** |

The det arms confirm the fix does exactly what it claims — **the convergence tolerance, which is
scaled by the norm of the right-hand side and is therefore a fingerprint of the initial state, is
now identical to the five digits printed at 288 and at 1152 ranks, where under legacy it differed
by a factor of two** — and the mesh still fails. Whatever blocks `fArc_sorted` is a property of that mesh, not of the
partition-dependent fill.

⇒ **Limitation 1(a) stands**, and can now be stated more sharply: the ladder stops at 2304 for a
reason that has been separated from the IC defect and remains un-diagnosed.

## 3a. 🔴 What the deterministic fill costs — INITIALISATION wall-clock, and nothing else

🔴 **First, what it does NOT cost. The fill is not compute.** It runs once during initialisation,
before the timestep loop, and never enters `s/step`. The measurement confirms it: standard-leg
step times match the legacy fleet to **within 0.4 % at every point**, fill included. No published
number is affected.

**The extra CG iterations are a start-up transient, not a standing cost.** An earlier draft of
this document claimed det "costs a few more CG iterations", from reading step 1 alone. Reading the
final step of each run instead:

| mesh | step 1, legacy → det | final step, legacy → det |
|---|---|---|
| DARS | 51 → 57 | **38 → 38** |
| fArc | 276 → 280 | **226 → 226** |
| NG5 | 85 → 97 | 84 → 86 |

The smoother field costs a few extra iterations while the model spins up and then costs nothing.
Only NG5 keeps a residual +2 on 84.

**What it does cost is SLURM walltime in a benchmark harness** — an operational fact about the
A/B job, not about the model. `job_m9_ab_*` restarts the model **six times per job** (three legs
× two reps), and **ten times** for a five-leg operating point, so it pays initialisation that
many times over. Production pays it once per cold start and **never on a restart**, because
`extrap_nod3D` only runs when the state is being interpolated from the PHC climatology.

The two fArc probes finished inside 95 s including their fills, and I took that as "the fill is
cheap" and trimmed the fleet's walltimes accordingly. **That was wrong, and it cost 15 jobs.**
Both probes ran at 288 and 1152 ranks. The fill is a Jacobi iteration to a tolerance, so its cost
scales *inversely* with rank count — each rank owns more of the mesh at low rank counts, and the
sweep count is fixed by the mesh. Measured as det elapsed minus the legacy twin's, over the six
model starts a three-leg job makes:

| per model start | 4 ranks | 8 | 16 | 32 | 64 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| CORE2 (127 k) | 23 s | 14 s | 10 s | 5 s | 2 s |
| fArc (638 k) | 74 s | 47 s | 30 s | 23 s | 10 s |
| DARS (3.2 M) | — | — | **202 s** | 139 s | 72 s |
| NG5 (7.4 M) | — | — | — | — | **148 s** |

On NG5 at 8 ranks the extrapolation is ~10 minutes per start — an hour of fill, six times over,
before the first timing.

⇒ **Practical rule, and it is about the harness, not the model: budget SLURM walltime for the
initialisation, times the number of model starts in the job.** For a scaling fleet the binding
constraint is the *smallest* rank count on the biggest mesh. Sizing from a high-rank probe
underestimates it by an order of magnitude. Resubmission driver with the full table:
`jobs/submit_m9_det_refill.sh`.

## 3b. An intermittent hang, recorded but NOT attributed to det

Three GPU jobs hung at **exactly the same place** — immediately after the `[cgpipe] built:` line,
i.e. at the first SSH solve of step 1, with the deterministic fill already complete (both its
lines present):

| job | tag | ranks |
|---|---|---|
| 26962782 | `scd_gpu_farc_8n` | 32 |
| 26961441 | `op6_farc` | 16 |
| 26969104 | `scd_gpu_ng5_8n` | 32 |

A common stall point is more suggestive than the node lists (which overlap only partially:
l50006, l50024 and l50039 appear in two of the three). But **it does not reproduce** — both
resubmissions completed, and quickly: `scd_gpu_farc_8n` in 5:01 and `op6_farc` in 9:02, well
inside walltimes they had previously exhausted.

Rate: 3 of 54 det GPU jobs (5.6 %) against 0 of 36 legacy GPU jobs. **That gap is not evidence.**
A 5.6 % background rate produces zero events in 36 trials about 12 % of the time, so the legacy
fleet's clean sheet is unremarkable, and a defect that vanishes on retry with the same binary and
the same knob is not a deterministic consequence of the fill. Recorded here so that a future
session seeing a fourth one has the pattern — first SSH solve, fill complete — rather than
starting from nothing.

## 4a. ⭐⭐ The premise, measured rather than assumed

The re-run rests on a claim about the *old* fleet: that its scaling curves compared points which
had not started from the same ocean. That is checkable directly, from logs that already exist —
the step-1 diagnostic row is a set of global reductions, so under identical initial conditions it
must be identical at every rank count.

🔴🔴 **Two traps in choosing the field. Each one produces a confident wrong answer, and I walked
into both before getting this right.**

1. **The global T and S extremes say "no problem anywhere."** They agree to the printed precision
   across every partition of every mesh — because the fill differs in the *marginal seas*, whose
   values sit inside the global range. Useless as a fingerprint.
2. **On CUDA most of the row is stale.** The diagnostics are assembled on the host and the
   device-resident fields are never mirrored back for them: `uv`, `w`, `vs`, `rs`, `bv`, `Kv`,
   `Av` all print as exactly `0.00e+00`, and `pgf`, `rho`, `hp` carry un-mirrored values. The
   same DARS 4-node configuration reads **`pgf=3.00e-04` on Serial and `8.20e-02` on CUDA**.
   ⇒ **`pgf` is a Serial-only fingerprint.** The fields that agree across backends, and are
   therefore usable on both, are the **CG iteration count** and **`eta`** — much blunter.

The sensitive field, where it is valid, is `pgf`: M13's mechanism is a density front across a
single element and `pgf` is what that front produces. `scripts/m9_det_vs_legacy.py` reports it:

| fleet | backend | mesh | pts | max\|pgf\| (Serial only) | eta | CG iters | verdict |
|---|---|---|---:|---|---|---|---|
| legacy | cpu | CORE2 | 3 | 1.73e-04 … 3.14e-04 — **1.82×** | 1.00× | 126–127 | partition-dependent |
| legacy | cpu | fArc | 5 | 2.35e-04 … 4.54e-04 — **1.93×** | 1.21× | 275–277 | partition-dependent |
| legacy | cpu | DARS | 4 | 1.73e-03 … 4.33e-03 — **2.50×** | 1.37× | 51–52 | partition-dependent |
| legacy | cpu | NG5 | 3 | 4.43e-04 … 1.03e-03 — **2.33×** | 1.02× | 84–85 | partition-dependent |
| legacy | gpu | DARS | 4 | — | **1.48×** | 51–52 | partition-dependent |
| legacy | gpu | NG5 | 4 | — | 1.00× | **85–86** | partition-dependent |
| legacy | gpu | CORE2 | 5 | — | 1.00× | 127 | *not detected* |
| legacy | gpu | fArc | 5 | — | 1.00× | 276 | *not detected* |
| **det** | cpu | CORE2 | 3 | 3.52e-05 — **1.00×** | 1.00× | 127 | flat |
| **det** | cpu | fArc | 5 | 4.65e-05 — **1.00×** | 1.00× | 280 | flat |
| **det** | cpu | DARS | 4 | 3.00e-04 — **1.00×** | 1.00× | 57 | flat |
| **det** | cpu | NG5 | 3 | 2.31e-04 — **1.00×** | 1.00× | 97 | flat |

**Six of the eight legacy curves are demonstrably partition-dependent; every det curve is flat.**
The remaining two — CORE2 and fArc on GPU — are **"not detected", not "clean"**: on CUDA the only
fields left are `eta` and the iteration count, and on the CPU arm of those same two meshes `pgf`
moves by 1.82× and 1.93× while `eta` moves by 1.00× and 1.21×. The GPU arm runs the identical
fill, so its ICs differ too; the instrument simply cannot see it there.

Two things worth keeping:

- ⭐ **The legacy fill was not a small perturbation.** Its step-1 `pgf` is **2 to 14× LARGER** than
  det's on every CPU mesh (DARS 1.73e-03…4.33e-03 against 3.00e-04). The old fill was injecting
  real spurious forcing into every cold start, not a decomposition-dependent rounding.
- **det's extra CG iterations are a START-UP TRANSIENT, not a standing cost.** At step 1 they read
  DARS 51–52 → 57, NG5 84–85 → 97, fArc 275–277 → 280; by the last step of the same runs they are
  DARS 38 → 38, fArc 226 → 226, NG5 84 → 86. See §3a — the step times are unaffected either way.

## 4b. Absent points — the det fleet reproduces the legacy fleet exactly

Four CPU legs produced no timing. All four are **out-of-memory at the smallest node counts**, and
all four failed the same way in the legacy fleet, so none of them is a det regression:

| point | ranks | det | legacy |
|---|---|---|---|
| `cpu_dars_1n` (+`_phst`) | 128 | OOM | OOM |
| `cpu_ng5_1n` | 128 | OOM | OOM |
| `cpu_ng5_2n` | 256 | OOM | OOM |

The det fill completes before the OOM in every case (the sweep-count lines are in the logs above
the kill), so the extra initialisation is not what exhausts the node.

🔴 **This makes one sentence of report Sect. 5.4 wrong, and it was already wrong before this
re-run**: *"Two intended CPU points are absent: DARS on one node exhausts memory … and NG5 on four
nodes exceeded its time limit and is being repeated."* There are **three** absent points, not two —
NG5 at one and two nodes are missing for the same memory reason as DARS — and the NG5 four-node
point is no longer being repeated; it landed (`sc_cpu_ng5_4n`, 4.4711 s/step). Fix both clauses
when the section is rewritten from the det fleet.

## 4c. CPU result (complete, 15 points, 4 meshes) — the published verdict is CONFIRMED

**Every det curve is flat in the fingerprint, and every legacy one is not:**

| mesh | legacy max\|pgf\| across the curve | det | CG iters, legacy → det |
|---|---|---|---|
| CORE2 | 1.73e-04 … 3.14e-04 (1.82×) | **3.52e-05 (1.00×)** | 126–127 → 127 |
| fArc | 2.35e-04 … 4.54e-04 (1.93×) | **4.65e-05 (1.00×)** | 275–277 → 280 |
| DARS | 1.73e-03 … 4.33e-03 (2.50×) | **3.00e-04 (1.00×)** | 51–52 → 57 |
| NG5 | 4.43e-04 … 1.03e-03 (2.33×) | **2.31e-04 (1.00×)** | 84–85 → 97 |

⭐ Note the *level*, not just the spread: det's step-1 `pgf` is **2 to 14 times smaller** than
legacy's on every mesh. The old fill was injecting real spurious forcing into every cold start,
not merely a decomposition-dependent rounding. It also costs a few more CG iterations on three of
the four meshes (NG5 worst, 85 → 97), which is the price of the smoother field.

**And none of it propagates into the CPU timings.** The published sentence barely moves:

| | points | wide halo vs standard, model step | … in the ice currency |
|---|---:|---|---|
| legacy | 15 | +2.4 … +22.0 % | +43 … +173 % |
| **det** | 15 | **+2.2 … +22.0 %** | **+43 … +172 %** |

Per-point: **28 step verdicts, 0 sign changes, largest shift 1.09 pp**; 27 ice verdicts, 0 sign
changes, largest shift 19.85 pp — but that is on a +151 % penalty, and the ice currency is the
noisier of the two (it comes from phasestats means, and the largest shifts sit at the smallest
per-rank workloads, moving in *opposite* directions on different meshes).

⇒ **The partition dependence was real and large in the forcing, and did not reach the CPU
result.** Sea ice still scales cleanly on CPU, the wide halo is still a uniform penalty at every
rank count tried, and the divergence form still leads. The published CPU verdict was not resting
on an artifact.

⚠️ While recomputing the ranges: report Sect. 5.4 says the penalty is *"between +46 and +173 %
… across all fourteen points measured, which is +2.9 to +22 % of the model step."* Recomputed
over the legacy fleet as it finally stands, it is **+43 … +173 %** over **fifteen** points, and
**+2.4 … +22.0 %** of the step. That discrepancy predates this re-run — the sentence was written
before the last point landed. Use the det numbers above when the section is rewritten.

## 6. Open, needs the user's word

The NG5 CPU curve stops at 2048 ranks (16 nodes) by this campaign's own convention. M13 showed
NG5 `dist_4096`, `dist_8192` and `dist_32768` were **fatal under legacy and clean under det**, so
those points are newly reachable and would extend the only curve on which sea ice is measured at
production rank counts. That is a 32–64 node CPU ask, past what this fleet has used, so it is
flagged rather than submitted.
