# M9 P1 pre-registration — the accuracy cost of the lagged mEVP halo (cell ⑤)

**Committed before the first accuracy leg runs.** Session 2, 2026-08-05.
Companion to `20260804-m9-PREREG.md` (the speed pre-registration, committed `bc85f02`).

---

## 0. Why this document exists

Session 1 measured cell ⑤ (`FESOM_SPEED_MEVPLAG=K`) at **−13 … −26 % of the GPU model step**
and reproduced it on the 638 k-node production mesh. It then failed to establish what the
approximation costs, and said so: `max|Δ|` at 300 steps showed **no monotone trend with K**,
with every large value resting on 1–5 points out of 126 858 — the signature of chaotic ice-edge
divergence, not of a K-dependent bias. A K=2 run showed a larger `a_ice` outlier than K=30.

So cell ⑤ is currently *a proven speed result with an unquantified accuracy cost*, and the
handoff blocks its promotion on this measurement. This document fixes the metrics, the
yardstick and the decision rule **before** any leg runs, so the verdict cannot be chosen after
seeing the numbers. Five consecutive M7 pre-registrations measured wrong-LOW (L93); the value of
pre-registering is not prediction accuracy, it is that the bar is not movable.

## 1. The question, stated so it can fail

> Does exchanging the mEVP halo every K-th subcycle instead of every subcycle change the
> simulated climate by **more than the difference between two independent correct
> implementations of mEVP itself**?

The comparison is deliberately not "is it small". It is "is it smaller than a difference the
project has already accepted as physics rather than error".

## 2. The yardstick — measured, pre-existing, not invented for this test

`docs/REFERENCE_RUNS.md:224-233`, measured 2026-07-12, year 1958, CORE2, C-port mEVP vs Fortran
mEVP — the same mesh, same config, same forcing, two independent implementations:

| field | sst | ssh | a_ice | m_ice | **uice** | **vice** |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **mEVP C↔F floor (correlation)** | 1.00000 | 1.00000 | 0.99999 | 0.99998 | **0.95438** | **0.93908** |

That `uice` floor is not sloppiness. mEVP is a fixed-point iteration (α=β=250 pseudo-steps) that
is only approximately converged, so two correct implementations disagree at ~0.95 on ice
velocity while agreeing at 1.00000 on SST. L79: **every scheme has its own floor, and reading
one scheme's floor onto another is how the fictitious "F↔C ice-edge budget" was born (L74).**

**Cell ⑤ is a change to how converged that same fixed-point iteration is.** So the floor is the
correct and the demanding yardstick: it is the noise the scheme already carries.

## 3. Decision rule — fixed now

Let `corr_K(f)` be the annual-mean pattern correlation of field `f` between the lag-K leg and the
classic leg, same backend, same rank count, same binary, same year.

- **PASS** — for every K ∈ {2, 4, 8} and every field `f`, `corr_K(f) ≥ floor(f)` from §2,
  on the CPU arm; and the NH/SH monthly ice-area and ice-extent series deviate from classic by
  no more than the classic leg itself deviates from the C-port mEVP reference.
- **FAIL** — any field falls below its floor at any K ≤ 8.
- **STRONG PASS** — additionally `1 − corr_K` is an order of magnitude smaller than `1 − floor`
  on the ice-velocity fields. That would say the approximation is far inside the scheme's own
  reproducibility, and is the outcome that would justify promoting the knob.

**Reported but explicitly NOT criteria:** `max|Δ|`. Session 1 established it cannot measure this
(§5 of the RESULTS doc). It is recorded for continuity and will be ignored in the verdict.
Bias and RMS are reported alongside correlation and are descriptive, not gating.

## 4. Controls — without these the legs mean nothing

1. **CPU classic replicate must be EXACTLY bit-identical to CPU classic.** The Serial backend at
   fixed rank count is deterministic (D22: atomics in edge order, one thread per rank). If this
   replicate is not byte-zero, the CPU arm is void and must be debugged before any lag-K number
   is read. This is the control that makes the CPU arm the arm of record.
2. **GPU classic replicate establishes the CUDA scatter-noise floor.** CUDA is *not*
   deterministic (D22: racing `atomic_add`, `parallel_reduce` reassociation), so a GPU lag-K
   difference is only interpretable against a GPU classic-vs-classic difference compounded over
   the same year. Pre-registered consequence: **if the GPU replicate noise is comparable to the
   GPU lag-K signal, the GPU arm cannot resolve the approximation and the CPU arm carries the
   verdict.** That is an anticipated outcome, not a failure.
3. **Knob-fired proof on every leg.** The `[m9] mEVP cell:` provenance line and the
   `[fesom_speed]` announce must appear, and `FESOM_SPEED_FORCE_SERIAL=1` must be set on CPU —
   without it every lever resolves OFF and the whole arm silently measures classic-vs-classic
   (L105a; it already cost this campaign one fleet).

## 6. Predictions, on the record

- **P1.1** The CPU classic replicate is byte-zero. *(If it is not, something is wrong with the
  determinism assumption and that is the finding.)*
- **P1.2** Scalar fields (sst, sss, ssh) stay at ≥ 0.9999 for all K ≤ 8 — the ice lever reaches
  the ocean only through surface stress and fluxes, over one year.
- **P1.3** `uice`/`vice` correlations land in **0.99–1.00**, i.e. roughly an order of magnitude
  inside the 0.954/0.939 floor. Reasoning: the lag is a delay in a halo of a contracting
  iteration (det1 = 250/251 < 1), so ring-1 dirt is bounded by K subcycles of a decaying
  signal — much milder than two implementations differing in their whole iteration.
- **P1.4** Any degradation is **monotone in K** in RMS terms even though it was not in `max|Δ|`,
  because RMS is not carried by 1–5 chaotic points.
- **P1.5** NH/SH ice area and extent are visually indistinguishable at all K ≤ 8.
- **P1.6** GPU replicate noise is **non-negligible** — CUDA compounds scatter noise over 17 280
  steps — and plausibly of the same order as the lag-K signal, per control 2.

Floors: 0. Prediction P1.3 is the one most likely to be wrong-optimistic; the pre-registered
consequence of `uice` landing *below* 0.954 is a FAIL and no promotion, not a re-argued bar.

## 7. What a PASS does and does not license

A PASS licenses exactly one thing: promoting `FESOM_SPEED_MEVPLAG` from an experimental knob to
a **documented opt-in option** with its accuracy characterised, on CORE2 at K ≤ 8.

It does **not** license: making it a default; K > 8 (untested, and §2 of RESULTS shows the speed
saturates at K ≈ 8–12 anyway, so there is nothing to buy); the high-resolution meshes
(ng5/farc accuracy is not measured here); or standard EVP (`whichEVP=0`), which the knob does
not touch. Those need their own legs, and cell ④ (P2) is the exact alternative that would need
no accuracy argument at all.
