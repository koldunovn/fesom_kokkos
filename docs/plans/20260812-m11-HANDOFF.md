## 2. The corrections. Read these before trusting anything

This session overturned five of its own claims. They generalise.

**2.1 The ladder dt (four separate incidents, L110).** Running a cold start at the *production*
dt makes the model diverge, and a campaign hunting bad partitions will book that as an arm result.
It cost three findings and, in the dars GPU case, **the baseline itself** — which made it read as
a machine fault for a day, because `srun: task N: Killed` is only teardown after `MPI_Abort` and
the real line (`CG_kk residual diverged`) is hundreds of lines earlier.
**CORE2 1800 · fArc 900 · dars 120 · NG5 180. Assert it in every job.**

**2.2 A 150-step race is not evidence (Finding 39).** The dars CPU "winner" raced −4.53 % over 150
steps and then diverged before step 3,000 while its baseline ran clean. Two of nine points now
carry this. `MINCONN` sometimes produces numerically fragile partitions and **nothing offline
predicts which** — the dying arm had the better 3-D balance, zero isolated nodes and a lower
edgecut than its own healthy re-roll. The remedy is real and cheap: **re-roll `FESOM_PART_SEED`**,
which has now rescued the arm on two different meshes (Findings 34, 41).

**2.3 Gates need an envelope, not a control (L112).** One control put an in-class arm at "2× the
class"; four controls span ×2.7 on that same statistic. And a control must be *demonstrated* to be
a different partition — mine coincided with base at 4 ranks (rms 5.9e−7). Use ≥3. Widening is not
a way to pass an arm: at fArc/16 GPU it *tightened* the envelope and the arms stayed out.

**2.4 `min-of-N` is biased when the baseline is noisier than the arms (Finding 43 correction).**
On dolpung the baseline is the noisiest leg, and min-of-N then rewards it: medians put every fArc
arm within 0.6 % of base, min-of-4 made them look 0.8–2.3 % slower off one lucky base rep.
**Report the median too whenever the spreads differ.** Related, and mine: I dropped the A100
winner from a tightened re-run on the strength of a measurement I had just called unresolvable.
If a result is unresolvable, it cannot justify dropping an arm.

**2.5 The scorecard is a design aid, never a gate** (Findings 18, 34, 37). It failed on four NG5
arms to identify a partition that destroys the run.

## 4. The science, as it currently stands

**GPU pays per message; CPU does not have a predictor.** Across all nine raced groups the
communication-partner count is the only metric family with a consistent positive correlation to
step time on GPU, while every volume-like metric correlates **negatively** — on GPU the arms that
ship more data are faster. No scorecard column orders CPU arms at all (Finding 37).

**The gain tracks the fractional reduction in `nbr_max`** — fArc −14 % → −3.6 %, CORE2 −33 % →
−8.1 %, dars −42 % → −18.6 %. By `nbr_mean` the three points are indistinguishable, so it is the
max that matters, which is right: the step waits for the slowest rank (Finding 43).

**The lever transfers to GH200 and grows there** — `MINCONN` −8.88 % vs A100's −8.06 %,
`MINCONN`+`CONTIG` −5.68 % vs −2.54 %, two independent jobs within 0.6 pp. dolpung has no
GPUDirect and stages every halo through pinned host, so a per-message lever should pay more, and
does (Finding 42). Good direction for JUPITER.

**Upstream**: `PartGraphRecursive` cannot use `OBJTYPE=VOL`, `CONTIG` or `MINCONN`, and `MINCONN`
is never set anywhere in `fort_part.c`. Word it that way — **not** "silently ignores": the authors
documented the trade-off and measured it on `mesh_aguv`. What changed is the hardware.
