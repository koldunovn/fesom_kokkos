{\large\bfseries Communication-avoiding solvers for the FESOM2 sea-surface-height system}\\[1pt]
{\small Interim report, 6 August 2026 --- FESOM2 C++/Kokkos port, Levante (A100 GPUs, 128-core CPU nodes)}

\textbf{What was done.}
The barotropic sea-surface-height (SSH) solve is the model's synchronisation bottleneck:
preconditioned CG performs two global reductions and two halo exchanges per iteration. We
implemented four alternatives behind a runtime switch --- Chronopoulos--Gear CG
(\texttt{cg2}), pipelined CG (\texttt{pipecg}), PIPECG-OATI (\texttt{oati}) and Chebyshev
P-CSI (\texttt{pcsi}) --- leaving the certified baseline bit-identical when the switch is
unset. Two defects had to be fixed first. The preconditioner inherited from FESOM's
\texttt{solver.F90} is not symmetric (defect ratio 0.62--0.74 over three meshes), which
invalidates the recurrence the CG--CG variants use in place of an explicit inner product: the
step length came out wrong by 21.8\,\% and most solves diverged. Symmetrising it also reduces
the \emph{existing} solver's iteration count by about 7\,\%. Our own stall guard was
separately aborting converging solves, its threshold of 20 non-improving iterations sitting
below the 21-iteration plateau baseline CG itself exhibits on fArc, so every previously
reported fArc failure was a false positive.

\textbf{Preliminary results.}
Each row is a same-allocation A/B against baseline CG, minimum of two repetitions, 300 steps,
zero fallback firings. $d_{\mathrm{SSH}}$ is the solver's own speed-up, $d_{\mathrm{tot}}$ the
whole-step speed-up; the latter reproduces to 0.4\,pp under a reversed leg order.

The SSH share of the step, not the mesh size or the per-core workload, predicts the payoff. It
rises as a mesh is spread thinner --- on CORE2 from 2.5\,\% at 128 ranks to 18.9\,\% at 864
--- so the solvers pay most where the model stops scaling, moving CORE2's parallel-efficiency
knee out by a factor of 1.26--1.29. On GPU the share is 27--31\,\% at ordinary node counts,
because the device makes local work fast while the solve stays latency-bound. Where the share
is small the variants lose, trading reductions for extra vector passes: a poor bargain when
the solve is 6\,\% of the step, as on DARS. \texttt{oati} is the better general choice and
\texttt{pcsi} a per-mesh specialist, since \texttt{oati} tracks baseline CG's iteration count
everywhere while \texttt{pcsi} converges faster only where its Chebyshev interval is accurate
--- an eigenvalue estimate that was silently wrong on fArc.

\textbf{Status and caveats.}
The fArc rows need the stall guard widened past its shipped default, and the value to ship has
not been chosen. The GPU rows predate that fix; fArc GPU is withdrawn pending re-measurement,
and NG5 has no valid CPU row, its tested rungs lying outside the operating range and its
baseline leg failing at 4096 ranks and above. DARS is measured at 300--500 vertices per core on newly
generated partitions, which form their own scaling curve. All numbers come from one machine
and a 300-step window, and the baseline solver remains the shipped default.
