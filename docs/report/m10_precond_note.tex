\section{Short answer}

Two questions were raised. Both have answers, and the second one is the interesting one.

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Is it the Kokkos port's bug?} No --- the port reproduces \texttt{solver.F90}
        term for term. The asymmetry is in the Fortran.
  \item \textbf{Should this not destroy plain CG as well?} No, and for a specific reason:
        plain PCG never uses the identity that fails. It loses optimality, not soundness ---
        measured cost \textbf{$\approx$7\,\% more iterations}, not divergence. The
        communication-avoiding variants have no such protection and diverge outright.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Where the asymmetry is}

\texttt{src/solver.F90}, in \texttt{ssh\_solve\_preconditioner}:

\begin{lstlisting}[language=Fortran]
pr_values(offset+1) = 1.0_WP/ssh_stiff%values(offset+1)
DO n=2, nend
   node = cind(offset+n)
   pr_values(n+offset) = -0.5_WP*(ssh_stiff%values(n+offset)/ssh_stiff%values(1+offset))/ &
                          (ssh_stiff%values(1+offset) + diag_values(node))
END DO
\end{lstlisting}

That is
\begin{equation}
  \mathrm{pr}[i,i]=\frac{1}{d_i},\qquad
  \mathrm{pr}[i,j]=-\frac{1}{2}\,\frac{a_{ij}/d_i}{d_i+d_j}
  =-\frac{1}{2}\,\frac{a_{ij}}{d_i\,(d_i+d_j)},\qquad d_i\equiv A_{ii}.
\end{equation}
The factor $(d_i+d_j)$ is symmetric and $a_{ij}=a_{ji}$, so \textbf{the only asymmetric part is
the leading $1/d_i$}: the transpose entry carries $1/d_j$ instead, giving
$\mathrm{pr}[i,j]/\mathrm{pr}[j,i]=d_j/d_i$.

Writing $\Mi=D^{-1}C$ with $D=\operatorname{diag}(A)$, $C_{ii}=1$ and
$C_{ij}=-\tfrac12 a_{ij}/(d_i+d_j)$: the matrix $C$ \emph{is} symmetric. The asymmetry sits
entirely in the left factor $D^{-1}$.

Measured on CORE2: $\max|M_{ij}-M_{ji}| / \max|M_{ij}| = \mathbf{0.638}$. The same measurement
on $A$ gives $1.4\times10^{-13}$, so the instrument is sound --- $A$ is symmetric, $\Mi$ is not.

\section{Why plain CG survives}

Plain PCG computes
\[
  \alpha_i=\frac{\gamma_i}{(p_i,Ap_i)}
\]
with an \textbf{explicit} dot product, and the orthogonality it actually depends on,
$(r_i,p_{i-1})=0$, holds \emph{whatever} $\Mi$ is. By induction, assuming
$(r_{i-1},p_{i-2})=0$:
\[
  (r_{i-1},p_{i-1}) = (r_{i-1},\,u_{i-1}+\beta_{i-1}p_{i-2}) = \gamma_{i-1},
  \qquad
  (r_i,p_{i-1}) = \gamma_{i-1} - \alpha_{i-1}(p_{i-1},Ap_{i-1}) = 0,
\]
with base case $p_0=u_0$. So $\alpha_i$ is always the exact line-search minimiser along $p_i$,
and PCG remains a monotone descent method: it cannot diverge. What symmetry of $\Mi$ buys is
\emph{full} conjugacy --- $r_i$ orthogonal to \emph{all} previous directions, not just the last
--- i.e.\ the optimality of the short recurrence. Without it, convergence is slower, not absent.

Measured, plain reference PCG on a real CORE2 system ($n_{\mathrm{nz}}=870\,146$), run to
convergence with each preconditioner:

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}lcc@{}}
\toprule
preconditioner & plain-PCG iterations & $\sigma$-recurrence drift\\
\midrule
as built (non-symmetric)               & \textbf{126} & $2.4\times10^{-1}$\\
symmetrised $D^{-1/2}CD^{-1/2}$        & \textbf{117} & $1.2\times10^{-13}$\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\noindent
\textbf{So the asymmetry costs the existing solver about 7\,\% in iterations} --- small enough
to have gone unnoticed, and nothing like a failure.

The Chronopoulos--Gear family is different. It \emph{replaces} the explicit $(p_i,Ap_i)$ with
the recurrence $\sigma_i=\delta_i-\beta_i^2\sigma_{i-1}$, whose derivation needs
$(r_i,M^{-\top}r_{i-1})=0$ --- i.e.\ exactly the symmetry that is missing. The error then
accumulates (21.8\,\% by iteration 60 on CORE2) with no line search to absorb it. In the model,
\texttt{cg2} on the as-built preconditioner triggers the fallback guard on \textbf{18 of 20
solves}, the residual growing to $2.9\times10^{4}$; with the symmetrised one it is clean.

\section{Suggested fix}

Keep the diagonal (already $1/d_i$, which is what $D^{-1/2}CD^{-1/2}$ gives) and scale each
off-diagonal by $\sqrt{d_i/d_j}$:
\begin{equation}
  \boxed{\ \mathrm{pr}[i,j]=-\frac{1}{2}\,\frac{a_{ij}}{\sqrt{d_i d_j}\,(d_i+d_j)}\ }
\end{equation}

\begin{lstlisting}[language=Fortran]
DO n=2, nend
   node = cind(offset+n)
   ! symmetric form: -0.5*a_ij / ( sqrt(d_i*d_j) * (d_i+d_j) )
   pr_values(n+offset) = -0.5_WP*ssh_stiff%values(n+offset)/                        &
                          ( sqrt(ssh_stiff%values(1+offset)*diag_values(node))      &
                            *(ssh_stiff%values(1+offset) + diag_values(node)) )
END DO
\end{lstlisting}

\noindent Three properties worth noting:

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Symmetric} by inspection ($a_{ij}=a_{ji}$, and both $\sqrt{d_id_j}$ and
        $d_i+d_j$ are symmetric).
  \item \textbf{Same spectrum.} $D^{-1/2}CD^{-1/2}=D^{1/2}(D^{-1}C)D^{-1/2}$ is a similarity
        transform of the present operator, so the eigenvalues are unchanged --- this is not a
        different preconditioner, it is the self-adjoint representative of the same one.
        (Checked numerically: $\lambda_{\max}$ agrees to ten significant figures.)
  \item \textbf{Free.} Same sparsity, built once at setup, no per-iteration cost. Nothing
        downstream changes; \texttt{diag\_values} is already halo-exchanged above.
\end{itemize}

\section{Caveats}

\begin{itemize}
  \item This is a \textbf{solver-class change to the production path}: the iterates differ
        within tolerance, so it deserves the usual convergence and climate check rather than a
        silent swap. We are holding our own variants to the same bar.
  \item $\sqrt{d_i d_j}$ is safe for realistic magnitudes here ($d\sim10^8$, so the product is
        $\sim10^{16}$, far inside double range). If that ever became a concern,
        $\sqrt{d_i}\sqrt{d_j}$ is equivalent and cannot overflow.
  \item The similarity argument is about $\Mi$ itself. The \emph{preconditioned} operator
        $\Mi A$ is not similar to the symmetrised one, and its extreme eigenvalue moves
        slightly (0.23\,\% on a variable-diagonal test problem) --- same convergence class, not
        the identical operator.
  \item If the intended MITgcm construction is symmetric, the discrepancy entered at the
        leading $1/d_i$ above, and it is worth checking against the original derivation ---
        the form suggested here is the minimal spectrum-preserving repair, not necessarily the
        one originally intended.
\end{itemize}
