{\large\bfseries Reply to S. Danilov's comments on the mEVP communication study}\\[0.2em]
{\small N. Koldunov, 6 August 2026}

\vspace{0.6em}\hrule\vspace{0.8em}

\textbf{On the intent of the divergence form.} Understood, and it works as intended. Our two
wide-halo variants differ only in what rides the window message, and on DARS at 32 GPUs the
divergence form removes \textbf{28.8\%} of the ice cost against \textbf{23.8\%} for the classic
form. Its advantage is ordered by how much mesh each GPU holds: it loses by 17 percentage points
on CORE2 at 32\,000 surface nodes per GPU, loses by 4 on fArc at 40\,000, and gains 5 on DARS at
99\,000. The halved, nodal message pays where messages are large, which is the regime it was aimed
at.

\textbf{Why our implementation exchanges more than yours.} Not for bit-identity in the first
instance, but because our ring is $K$ cells deep and rings $2\ldots K$ do not exist in FESOM's data
structures: on those nodes \texttt{a\_ice}, \texttt{u\_w}, \texttt{stress\_atmice} and the right-hand
sides are not last-bit different, they are absent. Your scheme never meets this because it stays
inside the halo FESOM already maintains, which is also what restricts it to $K=2$. At $K=2$ the two
approaches need the same data.

\textbf{Point 2 --- extending the computation over the $K-1$ ring.} You are right that this removes
the error and makes the sub-domain boundaries invisible, and we have measured it. Averaging
$|\Delta$ ice thickness$|$ on the sub-domain boundaries and in the interior over 60 days on fArc,
the ratio is 0.87 for the configuration that recomputes the ring, against 0.85 for two identical
runs; there is no imprint to detect. The configuration that does this is the one we call the wide
halo.

The difficulty is that the same change removes most of the saving:

\begin{center}\small
\begin{tabular}{@{}lrr@{}}
\toprule
GPU, four meshes at their operating points & ice cost & boundary/interior ratio \\
\midrule
delayed exchange, stale halo, no ring computation & $-37$ to $-58\%$ & 1.70 ($K$=2), 2.52 ($K$=4) \\
ring recomputed (our wide halo) & $-11$ to $-29\%$ & 0.87 \\
two identical runs & --- & 0.85 \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

The delayed exchange is about three times faster than the wide halo \emph{because} it does not
compute in the ring, and computing in the ring is what makes the boundaries invisible. The imprint
and the saving have the same origin, so a scheme that extends the computation over the ring
converges onto the wide halo and inherits its numbers. We have not found an intermediate that is
both cheap and clean, and we would be glad to be shown one.

\textbf{Point 1 --- what sacrificing exactness would buy.} Part of this we can bound and part we
cannot.

The auxiliary fields we ship once per model step, which is the cost you object to, amount to
1.6--6.2\% of the wide halo's messages and 3.6--9.2\% of the data it moves, because they are sent
once per step against $120/K$ window exchanges. Removing them altogether therefore gains at most
about 0.4 percentage points of the model step. On this evidence the field shipping is not what
holds the scheme back.

What we have not measured is the second half. Our implementation performs the ring computation in
kernels of its own --- two extra per sub-cycle in the classic form and three in the divergence
form, so 240 to 360 more per model step --- whereas yours simply extends the bounds of the loops
that already exist and adds none. Of the 8.2\,ms ice-dynamics phase we profiled, only 1.25\,ms is
arithmetic, spread over some 480 launches per step, so that is where a substantial gain could still
be hiding. We cannot bound it from the runs we have.

\textbf{What we propose.} We will build the variant you describe --- the ring computation folded
into the loops that already exist, in the natural order, exchanging velocities and stress
divergence every $K$ sub-cycles and recomputing the rest --- and measure it against our wide halo
at the same operating points. The ring computation is common to both, so the difference between
them should be the launches and the ordering machinery, which your formulation avoids. We will
report the result whichever way it comes out.
