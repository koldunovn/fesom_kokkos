{\Large\bfseries Sea-ice sub-cycling in the FESOM2 GPU port: what pays, and what it costs}\\[0.2em]
{\small N. Koldunov, 6 August 2026 --- summary of the mEVP communication study}

\vspace{0.4em}\hrule\vspace{0.5em}

\hd{Three ways to stop exchanging the halo 120 times per step}

mEVP runs 120 sub-cycles per model time step and currently exchanges the ice-velocity halo after
every one. Three ways to do it less often were implemented and measured:

\textbf{delayed exchange} --- exchange every $K$-th sub-cycle and let the halo go stale. Fastest by
far, \emph{but it imprints the domain decomposition on the ice} (below).
\textbf{Wide halo} (Danilov) --- exchange every $K$-th sub-cycle but recompute a $K$-deep ghost
ring in between, so nothing is stale: \emph{exact}, bit for bit, and no imprint; smaller gain.
\textbf{Divergence form} (Danilov) --- carry $\nabla\!\cdot\!\sigma$ at nodes instead of $\sigma$
at elements: exact to $10^{-15}$, pays on CPU, costs on GPU.

\hd{What they buy --- in both currencies}

Percent of the \emph{model step} is what a production run feels; percent of the \emph{ice cost}
(ice + ice dynamics + ice advection, compute \emph{and} MPI wait) is what judges a sea-ice scheme.
They differ 5--10$\times$, and quoting only the first makes a good scheme look like nothing.

\begin{center}\small
\begin{tabular}{@{}llrrrr@{}}
\toprule
& & \multicolumn{2}{c}{\textbf{GPU}} & \multicolumn{2}{c}{\textbf{CPU, at scale}} \\
\cmidrule(lr){3-4}\cmidrule(lr){5-6}
& & ice cost & model step & ice cost & model step \\
\midrule
delayed exchange & $K$=4 & $-37$ to $-58\%$ & $-8.5$ to $-14\%$ & $-5$ to $-25\%$ & $-0.3$ to $-3.6\%$ \\
wide halo, exact & $K$=8 & $-12$ to $-19\%$ & $-2$ to $-7\%$ & \multicolumn{2}{c}{never wins} \\
divergence form alone & --- & $+2$ to $+17\%$ & $0$ to $+1.6\%$ & $-2$ to $-8.5\%$ & $-0.5$ to $0\%$ \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

The CPU columns grow with core count ($-5\%$ of the ice at 128 ranks, $-25\%$ at 2048); the GPU
columns shrink with the mesh a GPU holds ($-58\%$ at 32\,k nodes per GPU, $-37\%$ at 460\,k).
The divergence form is \textbf{not} a null result: on CPU at 512 ranks and above it takes 2--8.5\%
off the ice and is simply invisible in the model step. On GPU it costs, because there the sub-cycle
is limited by the number of GPU operations issued rather than by arithmetic or memory traffic, so
removing element arrays removes work that was not being paid for. Danilov's own $\sim$20\% for the
divergence form with its double halo matches our \textbf{$-19.1\%$ of the ice cost} for that
combination (fArc, 16 GPU nodes).

\hd{\textcolor{red}{The finding that decides the recommendation: the fast option imprints the partition}}

The delayed exchange reads halo values up to $K-1$ sub-cycles old, and those nodes are exactly the
one-cell rim of each sub-domain. The partition is static, so the same nodes are perturbed every
step of the run. Measured on fArc, 16 GPUs, 60 days of daily output --- mean $|\Delta$ ice
thickness$|$ on the rim against the interior:

\begin{center}\small
\begin{tabular}{@{}lrrr@{}}
\toprule
& rim & interior & \textbf{ratio} \\
\midrule
two identical runs (control --- no lever at all) & 0.00105\,m & 0.00124\,m & \textbf{0.85} \\
\textbf{wide halo, exact, $K$=8} & 0.00104 & 0.00120 & \textbf{0.87} \\
delayed exchange, $K$=2 & 0.00432 & 0.00249 & \textbf{1.70} \\
delayed exchange, $K$=4 & 0.01178 & 0.00452 & \textbf{2.52} \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

It is there on \textbf{day one} (ratio 3.1), persists for two months, scales with $K$, and traces
the sub-domain boundaries visibly. The exact wide halo scores like the control, as it must, which
also validates the diagnostic. \textbf{The delayed exchange therefore makes the answer depend on
how the mesh was partitioned.} The ring profiles and the map are Figure~4 of the full note.

\hd{What the delayed exchange costs in simulated climate (1 year, CORE2, exactly reproducible)}

Pattern correlation against the same run exchanging every sub-cycle, judged against the difference
between two independent correct implementations of mEVP (our C port versus Fortran FESOM2). At
$K$=4: ice concentration 0.999995 and thickness 0.999994 against a yardstick of 0.99999/0.99998;
ice velocity 0.963/0.950 against 0.954/0.939. Every field stays inside the yardstick at $K\le8$,
but only by 1.1--1.5$\times$ on ice velocity. Hemispheric ice extent moves 0.23\% of its seasonal
amplitude and the ocean surface is unchanged to six digits. \emph{CORE2 resolution only.}

\hd{Recommendation}

\textbf{For production, the exact wide halo.} Bit-for-bit identical to the standard scheme, so no
accuracy argument and no partition imprint, and worth 12--19\% of the ice cost on GPU at $K$=8. It
does not pay on CPU on any mesh.

\textbf{The delayed exchange is not recommended as it stands}, despite being worth three times as
much: results stop being reproducible across core counts and the error is organised along
artificial lines. If the speed is wanted, establish whether the imprint matters over a decade, or
whether a hybrid (short delay, occasional exact refresh) removes it.

\textbf{The divergence form} is worth having on CPU at scale and is a cost on GPU --- and it is
what makes the wide halo cheapest to carry.
