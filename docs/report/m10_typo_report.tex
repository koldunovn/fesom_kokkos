\section{What this is, and the one result that matters}

Four communication-avoiding solvers for the FESOM2 sea-surface-height system were
implemented for the Kokkos port. Rather than transcribe the published listings, each
was re-derived from first principles and then compared against the papers and against
the existing Fortran implementation. Nine discrepancies came out of that comparison;
they are tabulated in \S\ref{sec:table}.

One of them is not a typo but a missing hypothesis, and it matters enough to lead with:

\begin{quote}
\textbf{The $\alpha$ recurrence used by every Chronopoulos--Gear variant is valid only
when the preconditioner is symmetric. None of the sources say so. The FESOM
MITgcm-class preconditioner is not symmetric, and the resulting error in the step
length reaches $21.8\,\%$ on the production CORE2 matrix.}
\end{quote}

\noindent
Running \texttt{cg2} with the preconditioner as built makes the solver diverge on 18 of
20 solves. Symmetrising it --- a one-line change that provably preserves the
preconditioner's spectrum --- removes the divergence entirely. This reproduces and
explains the \texttt{cg2} instability that had been observed previously.
\S\ref{sec:main} gives the derivation, the measurements and the remedy.

\section{The unstated symmetry hypothesis}
\label{sec:main}

\subsection{What the code builds}

\texttt{fesom\_ssh\_preconditioner} constructs, per row $i$,
\begin{equation}
  \mathrm{pr}[i,i]=\frac{1}{d_i},\qquad
  \mathrm{pr}[i,j]=-\frac{1}{2}\,\frac{a_{ij}/d_i}{d_i+d_j}\quad (j\neq i),
  \qquad d_i \equiv A_{ii}.
\end{equation}
The transpose entry carries $d_j$ in the leading division, so
$\mathrm{pr}[i,j]/\mathrm{pr}[j,i]=d_j/d_i$ and $\Mi\neq(\Mi)^{\!\top}$ wherever
$d_i\neq d_j$. On an unstructured ocean mesh the diagonal varies with cell area and
depth, so this is structural, not a small perturbation. Measured on CORE2:

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}lccc@{}}
\toprule
operator & $\max|X_{ij}-X_{ji}|$ & $\max|X_{ij}|$ & defect ratio\\
\midrule
$\Mi$ (\texttt{pr\_values}) & $8.561\times10^{-8}$ & $1.342\times10^{-7}$ & $\mathbf{0.638}$\\
$A$ (control)               & $2.150\times10^{-5}$ & $1.510\times10^{8}$  & $1.42\times10^{-13}$\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\subsection{Why the baseline PCG does not care, and the variants do}

Baseline PCG forms $\alpha_i=\gamma_i/(p_i,Ap_i)$ with an explicit dot product every
iteration. Nothing is recurred, no orthogonality identity is invoked, and it converges
normally. The Chronopoulos--Gear family replaces that explicit quantity with
\begin{equation}
  \sigma_i=\delta_i-\beta_i^2\sigma_{i-1},\qquad \alpha_i=\gamma_i/\sigma_i,
  \qquad \sigma_i\equiv(p_i,Ap_i).
  \label{eq:sigma}
\end{equation}
With $p_i=u_i+\beta_i p_{i-1}$ and $A$ symmetric,
$(p_i,Ap_i)=\delta_i+2\beta_i(u_i,s_{i-1})+\beta_i^2\sigma_{i-1}$ where
$s_{i-1}=Ap_{i-1}$, so \eqref{eq:sigma} holds if and only if
\begin{equation}
  (u_i,s_{i-1}) = -\beta_i\sigma_{i-1}.
  \label{eq:star}
\end{equation}
Using $s_{i-1}=(r_{i-1}-r_i)/\alpha_{i-1}$,
\begin{equation}
  (u_i,s_{i-1})=\frac{(\Mi r_i,\,r_{i-1})-\gamma_i}{\alpha_{i-1}},
  \qquad
  (\Mi r_i,r_{i-1}) = (r_i,\,M^{-\top}r_{i-1}).
\end{equation}
That term vanishes by the standard PCG residual $M$-orthogonality \emph{only when}
$M^{-\top}=\Mi$; then $(u_i,s_{i-1})=-\gamma_i/\alpha_{i-1}=-\beta_i\sigma_{i-1}$,
which is \eqref{eq:star}. With a non-symmetric $\Mi$ it does not vanish,
\eqref{eq:star} fails, and $\sigma_i$ drifts from the true $(p_i,Ap_i)$ --- cumulatively,
because $\sigma$ is a recurrence. A wrong $\sigma$ gives a wrong $\alpha$, which is no
longer the line-search minimiser: the method stops being CG.

\eqref{eq:sigma} through a reference PCG solve on a dumped CORE2 system

symmetrised form of \S\ref{sec:fix}:

The quantity required to vanish instead reaches $24\,\%$ of $\gamma$, and the step length
is wrong by up to $22\,\%$ --- at the production tolerance, on the production matrix.
In the model, \texttt{cg2} with the literal preconditioner fires the auto-fallback guard
on \textbf{18 of 20 solves}, the residual growing to $2.9\times10^{4}$ against a tolerance
of $\approx 4$. With the symmetrised preconditioner: \textbf{no fallbacks}, 123.35
iterations per solve. Independently, the $\alpha$ sequence departs from reference PCG by
$2.017\,\%$ at iteration~2, matching the measured $\sigma$ drift of $1.977\,\%$ term for
term.

\subsection{Remedy}
\label{sec:fix}

Factor the preconditioner as $\Mi=D^{-1}C$ with $D=\operatorname{diag}(A)$, $C_{ii}=1$,
$C_{ij}=-\tfrac12 a_{ij}/(d_i+d_j)$. Since $a_{ij}=a_{ji}$, the matrix $C$ \emph{is}
symmetric --- the whole asymmetry sits in the left factor $D^{-1}$. Define
\begin{equation}
  \Mt := D^{-1/2}\,C\,D^{-1/2}
  \qquad\text{i.e.}\qquad
  \widetilde{\mathrm{pr}}[i,j]=\mathrm{pr}[i,j]\sqrt{d_i/d_j},
  \quad \widetilde{\mathrm{pr}}[i,i]=\mathrm{pr}[i,i].
\end{equation}
$\Mt$ is symmetric by construction, and it is \emph{similar} to $\Mi$ because
$D^{1/2}(D^{-1}C)D^{-1/2}=D^{-1/2}CD^{-1/2}$. Similar matrices share their spectrum, so
this is exactly the same quality of approximate inverse --- symmetry is not being bought
at the price of conditioning. (Verified: $\lambda_{\max}(\Mi)$ and $\lambda_{\max}(\Mt)$
agree to ten significant figures.) Cost is one multiplication per stored entry, applied
once at setup; sparsity and all halo machinery are unchanged.

One caveat worth stating: the similarity is a statement about $\Mi$, not about the
preconditioned operator. $\Mt A$ is not similar to $\Mi A$, and its extreme eigenvalue
shifts by $0.23\,\%$ on a variable-diagonal test problem. Same convergence class, not the
identical operator.

\textbf{T-1} & \src{[CV]} Alg.~4; \src{[S]}; \src{[T]} &
The $\alpha$ recurrence $\sigma_i=\delta_i-\beta_i^2\sigma_{i-1}$ is stated without its

``SPD preconditioner'' in prose; the FESOM \texttt{pr\_values} is not symmetric (defect ratio
$0.638$). Measured: $\alpha$ wrong by up to $21.8\,\%$ on CORE2, \texttt{cg2} diverging on 18

\textbf{T-2} & \src{[P]} App.~B2 &
Line 4 computes $\sigma_k$ using $\beta_k$, which is only defined on line 5 --- the listing is
not executable in the order printed. Harmless in effect ($\sigma_0=0$ makes the undefined
$\beta_1$ multiply zero on the first pass), but from $k\geq2$ the reader must reorder.
Similarly $\rho_0=1$ gives $\beta_1=\rho_1$ rather than $0$, harmless only because
$s_0=p_0=0$. & \sev{S2}\\[2pt]

\textbf{T-3} & \src{[T]} Alg.~4, l.~11 &
The $\gamma_{i+1}$ recurrence folds $(r_i,q_i)$ and $(s_i,u_i)$ into one $2\alpha_i(\cdot)$
term. That identification needs $(r_i,\Mi w_i)=(\Mi r_i,w_i)$, i.e.\ a symmetric $\Mi$ ---
the same unstated hypothesis as T-1. & \sev{S1}\\[2pt]

\textbf{T-4} & \src{[T]} Alg.~4, l.~12 &
$\delta_{i+1}=\lambda_8-\alpha_i(\lambda_1{+}\beta_i\lambda_2)-\alpha_i(\lambda_1{+}\beta_i\lambda_2)+\dots$
--- the identical product printed twice instead of $2\alpha_i(\cdot)$. Arithmetically the same,
but it conceals that the two terms come from \emph{different} inner products which coincide
only under a symmetric $\Mi$. & \sev{S3}\\[2pt]

\textbf{T-5} & \src{[P]} App.~C &
The Lanczos start vector is printed as $q_1=r_0/(r_0^{\top}s_0)$: normalised by the inner
product rather than by its \textbf{square root}. The missing root corrupts $\alpha_1$ and
plants a spurious small eigenvalue in $T$. On a 1024-unknown fixture $\theta_{\min}$ comes out
as $2.49\times10^{-6}$ instead of $5.65\times10^{-3}$ --- \textbf{2270$\times$ too small}, and
it does not wash out with $m$ (same error at $m=30$ and $m=120$). Feeding that $\nu$ to P-CSI
inflates the assumed condition number $\sim$2000$\times$, so the Chebyshev polynomial is
optimised for a far too wide interval and convergence is much slower than it should be ---
silently, since it still converges. & \sev{S1}\\[2pt]

\textbf{T-6} & \src{[P]} App.~B3, l.~1 &
The $\omega$ recurrence coefficient is typeset as a fraction readable as either
$\tfrac14\alpha^2$ or $1/(4\alpha^2)$. \emph{Not a defect in the paper} --- a transcription
hazard. Resolved to $1/(4\alpha^2)$ three ways (\S\ref{sec:omega}). Recorded so it need not be
re-litigated. & \sev{S1}\newline\emph{if misread}\\[2pt]

\textbf{T-7} & \src{[T]} \S2.2 &
The method is designed and benchmarked with \textbf{Jacobi} (diagonal) preconditioning, where
$\Mi$ needs no halo exchange. Not a typo, but a portability caveat: with a sparse $\Mi$

$n\to g\to h\to e\to f$ chain becomes four chained applications, and the exchanged halo volume
roughly doubles. & \sev{S2}\\[2pt]

\textbf{T-8} & \src{[S]} both routines &

\section{Resolution of the P-CSI \texorpdfstring{$\omega$}{omega} recurrence (T-6)}
\label{sec:omega}

Let the preconditioned operator have spectrum in $[\nu,\mu]$ and set
$\theta=\tfrac12(\mu+\nu)$, $\delta_{\mathrm{h}}=\tfrac12(\mu-\nu)$,
$\sigma_1=\theta/\delta_{\mathrm{h}}$. The standard three-term form \src{[GV]} is
$\rho_0=1/\sigma_1$, $\rho_k=1/(2\sigma_1-\rho_{k-1})$,
$\Delta x_k=\rho_k\rho_{k-1}\Delta x_{k-1}+(2\rho_k/\delta_{\mathrm{h}})\Mi r_k$. With the
paper's variables $\alpha=2/(\mu-\nu)=1/\delta_{\mathrm{h}}$ and $\gamma=\theta$, put
$\omega_k:=2\rho_k/\delta_{\mathrm{h}}$. Then
\begin{equation}
  \omega_k=\frac{2}{\delta_{\mathrm{h}}(2\sigma_1-\rho_{k-1})}
         =\frac{2}{2\theta-\delta_{\mathrm{h}}\rho_{k-1}},
  \qquad \rho_{k-1}=\tfrac12\delta_{\mathrm{h}}\omega_{k-1},
\end{equation}
so that, using $\delta_{\mathrm{h}}=1/\alpha$,
\begin{equation}
  \omega_k=\frac{1}{\theta-\tfrac14\delta_{\mathrm{h}}^{2}\,\omega_{k-1}}
         =\frac{1}{\gamma-\dfrac{\omega_{k-1}}{4\alpha^{2}}}.
\end{equation}
The reading $\tfrac14\alpha^2$ is therefore incorrect. Two independent confirmations: the
paper's own seed $\omega_0=2/\gamma$ follows from $\rho_0=1/\sigma_1$ only under this reading;
and numerically, the derived coefficient meets the Golub--Varga convergence bound at
$k=10,20,40$ while the misread one does not. Note also that the initial step uses
$\Delta x_0=\gamma^{-1}\Mi r_0$, \emph{not} $\omega_0$ --- a reader assuming otherwise is off
by exactly a factor of two on the first step.
