\begin{abstract}
\noindent
Four communication-avoiding solvers for the FESOM2 sea-surface-height (SSH) system
--- Chronopoulos--Gear CG (\texttt{cg2}), pipelined PCG (\texttt{pipecg}),
PIPECG-OATI (\texttt{oati}) and the preconditioned Chebyshev/Stiefel iteration
(\texttt{pcsi}) --- were re-derived from first principles rather than transcribed,
then cross-checked against three published listings and one existing Fortran
implementation. Nine discrepancies were found. The most consequential is that the
$\alpha$ recurrence shared by the whole Chronopoulos--Gear family is valid
\emph{only} for a symmetric preconditioner, a hypothesis stated in none of the
sources; the FESOM MITgcm-class preconditioner is not symmetric, and the resulting
error in the step length reaches $21.8\,\%$ on the production CORE2 matrix. This
reproduces, and explains, an instability previously observed with \texttt{cg2}.
A one-line remedy is given and verified.
\end{abstract}

Notation: $A$ is the SSH stiffness matrix (symmetric to $1.4\times10^{-13}$,

\section{The principal finding: an unstated symmetry hypothesis}
\label{sec:main}

\subsection{What the code builds}

\texttt{fesom\_ssh\_preconditioner} constructs, per row $i$,
\begin{equation}
  \mathrm{pr}[i,i]=\frac{1}{d_i},\qquad
  \mathrm{pr}[i,j]=-\frac{1}{2}\,\frac{a_{ij}/d_i}{d_i+d_j}\quad (j\neq i),
  \qquad d_i \equiv A_{ii}.
\end{equation}
The transpose entry carries $d_j$ in the leading division, hence
\begin{equation}
  \frac{\mathrm{pr}[i,j]}{\mathrm{pr}[j,i]}=\frac{d_j}{d_i}
  \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \Mi \neq (\Mi)^{\!\top}
  \ \ \text{wherever } d_i\neq d_j .
\end{equation}
On an unstructured ocean mesh the diagonal varies with cell area and depth, so this
is a structural property, not a small perturbation. Measured on the CORE2 matrix:

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}lccc@{}}
\toprule
operator & $\max|X_{ij}-X_{ji}|$ & $\max|X_{ij}|$ & \textbf{defect ratio}\\
\midrule
$\Mi$ (\texttt{pr\_values}) & $8.561\times10^{-8}$ & $1.342\times10^{-7}$ & $\mathbf{0.638}$\\
$A$ (control)               & $2.150\times10^{-5}$ & $1.510\times10^{8}$  & $1.42\times10^{-13}$\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\subsection{Why the baseline PCG is unaffected}

Baseline PCG forms $\alpha_i=\gamma_i/(p_i,Ap_i)$ with an \emph{explicit} dot product
every iteration. Nothing is recurred, so no orthogonality identity is invoked, and the
method converges normally.

\subsection{Why every Chronopoulos--Gear variant is affected}

The family replaces that explicit quantity by the recurrence
\begin{equation}
  \sigma_i=\delta_i-\beta_i^2\sigma_{i-1},\qquad \alpha_i=\gamma_i/\sigma_i,
  \qquad \sigma_i\equiv(p_i,Ap_i).
  \label{eq:sigma}
\end{equation}
With $p_i=u_i+\beta_i p_{i-1}$ and $A$ symmetric,
\begin{equation}
  (p_i,Ap_i)=\delta_i+2\beta_i(u_i,s_{i-1})+\beta_i^2\sigma_{i-1},
  \qquad s_{i-1}=Ap_{i-1},
\end{equation}
so \eqref{eq:sigma} holds \emph{if and only if}
\begin{equation}
  (u_i,s_{i-1}) = -\beta_i\sigma_{i-1}.
  \label{eq:star}
\end{equation}
Using $s_{i-1}=(r_{i-1}-r_i)/\alpha_{i-1}$,
\begin{equation}
  (u_i,s_{i-1})=\frac{(\Mi r_i,\,r_{i-1})-\gamma_i}{\alpha_{i-1}},
  \qquad
  (\Mi r_i,r_{i-1}) = (r_i,\,M^{-\top}r_{i-1}).
\end{equation}
That last term vanishes by the standard PCG residual $M$-orthogonality \emph{only
when} $M^{-\top}=\Mi$; then
$(u_i,s_{i-1})=-\gamma_i/\alpha_{i-1}=-\beta_i\sigma_{i-1}$, which is
\eqref{eq:star}. With a non-symmetric $\Mi$ the term does not vanish,
\eqref{eq:star} fails, and $\sigma_i$ drifts from the true $(p_i,Ap_i)$
cumulatively, because $\sigma$ is a recurrence. A wrong $\sigma$ gives a wrong
$\alpha$, which is no longer the line-search minimiser: the method stops being CG.

\subsection{Measured consequence}

A diagnostic replay carries both the true $(p_i,Ap_i)$ and the recurrence
\eqref{eq:sigma} through a reference PCG solve on a dumped CORE2 system
($n_{\mathrm{nz}}=870\,146$), first with the preconditioner as built and then with
the symmetrised form of \S\ref{sec:fix}:

\noindent
The quantity required to vanish instead reaches $24\,\%$ of $\gamma$, and the step
length is wrong by up to $22\,\%$ --- at the production tolerance, on the production
matrix. Running \texttt{cg2} in the model with the literal preconditioner fires the
auto-fallback guard on \textbf{18 of 20 solves}, the residual growing to
$2.9\times10^{4}$ against a tolerance of $\approx 4$. With the symmetrised
preconditioner: \textbf{zero fallbacks}, $123.35$ iterations per solve. Independently,
the $\alpha$ sequence departs from reference PCG by $2.017\,\%$ at iteration~2,
matching the measured $\sigma$ drift of $1.977\,\%$ term for term.

\subsection{Remedy}
\label{sec:fix}

Factor the preconditioner as $\Mi=D^{-1}C$ with $D=\operatorname{diag}(A)$,
$C_{ii}=1$ and $C_{ij}=-\tfrac12 a_{ij}/(d_i+d_j)$. Since $a_{ij}=a_{ji}$, the matrix
$C$ \emph{is} symmetric --- the entire asymmetry sits in the left factor $D^{-1}$.
Define
\begin{equation}
  \boxed{\ \Mt := D^{-1/2}\,C\,D^{-1/2}\ }
  \qquad\text{i.e.}\qquad
  \widetilde{\mathrm{pr}}[i,j]=\mathrm{pr}[i,j]\sqrt{d_i/d_j},
  \quad \widetilde{\mathrm{pr}}[i,i]=\mathrm{pr}[i,i].
\end{equation}
Two properties make this the right object:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item $\Mt$ is symmetric by construction.
  \item $\Mt$ is \emph{similar} to $\Mi$, since
        $D^{1/2}(D^{-1}C)D^{-1/2}=D^{-1/2}CD^{-1/2}$. Similar matrices share their
        spectrum, so $\Mt$ is exactly the same quality of approximate inverse.
        (Verified: $\lambda_{\max}(\Mi)$ and $\lambda_{\max}(\Mt)$ agree to ten
        significant figures.)
\end{enumerate}
Cost: one multiplication per stored entry, applied once at setup; the sparsity
pattern and all halo machinery are unchanged.

\medskip
\noindent\emph{Caveat.} The similarity is a statement about $\Mi$, not about the
preconditioned operator: $\Mt A$ is not similar to $\Mi A$, and its extreme
eigenvalue shifts by $0.23\,\%$ on a variable-diagonal test problem. Same
convergence class, not the identical operator.

\textbf{T-1} & \src{[CV]} Alg.~4; \src{[S]}; \src{[T]} &
The $\alpha$ recurrence $\sigma_i=\delta_i-\beta_i^2\sigma_{i-1}$ is stated without its
hypothesis. It is valid only for a symmetric $\Mi$ (\S\ref{sec:main}). The sources assume
an ``SPD preconditioner'' in prose; the FESOM MITgcm-class \texttt{pr\_values} is not
symmetric (defect ratio $0.638$). Measured consequence: $\alpha$ wrong by up to $21.8\,\%$
on CORE2, and \texttt{cg2} diverges on 18 of 20 solves. & \sev{S1}\\[2pt]

\textbf{T-2} & \src{[P]} App.~B2 &
Line 4 computes $\sigma_k$ using $\beta_k$, which is only defined on line 5: the listing is
not executable in the order printed. Harmless in effect ($\sigma_0=0$ makes the undefined
$\beta_1$ multiply zero on the first pass), but the reader must reorder from $k\geq2$.
Likewise $\rho_0=1$ gives $\beta_1=\rho_1$ rather than $0$, harmless only because
$s_0=p_0=0$. & \sev{S2}\\[2pt]

\textbf{T-3} & \src{[T]} Alg.~4, l.~11 &
The $\gamma_{i+1}$ recurrence folds $(r_i,q_i)$ and $(s_i,u_i)$ into a single
$2\alpha_i(\cdot)$ term. That identification requires $(r_i,\Mi w_i)=(\Mi r_i,w_i)$,
i.e.\ a symmetric $\Mi$ --- the same unstated hypothesis as T-1. & \sev{S1}\\[2pt]

\textbf{T-4} & \src{[T]} Alg.~4, l.~12 &
$\delta_{i+1}=\lambda_8-\alpha_i(\lambda_1{+}\beta_i\lambda_2)-\alpha_i(\lambda_1{+}\beta_i\lambda_2)+\dots$
--- the identical product is printed twice instead of $2\alpha_i(\cdot)$. Arithmetically
equivalent, but it conceals that the two terms arise from \emph{different} inner products
which coincide only under a symmetric $\Mi$. & \sev{S3}\\[2pt]

\textbf{T-5} & \src{[P]} App.~C &
The Lanczos start vector is printed as $q_1=r_0/(r_0^{\top}s_0)$: normalisation by the
inner product rather than by its \textbf{square root}. The missing root corrupts $\alpha_1$
and plants a spurious small eigenvalue in $T$. Measured on a 1024-unknown fixture:
$\theta_{\min}$ returns $2.49\times10^{-6}$ instead of $5.65\times10^{-3}$ ---
\textbf{2270$\times$ too small}, and it does \emph{not} wash out with $m$ (identical error at
$m=30$ and $m=120$). Feeding that $\nu$ to P-CSI inflates the assumed condition number by
$\sim$2000$\times$, so the Chebyshev polynomial is optimised for a far too wide interval and
the method converges much more slowly than it should --- silently, since it still converges.
& \sev{S1}\\[2pt]

\textbf{T-6} & \src{[P]} App.~B3, l.~1 &
The $\omega$ recurrence coefficient is typeset as a fraction that can be read as either
$\tfrac14\alpha^2$ or $1/(4\alpha^2)$. \emph{Not a defect in the paper} --- a transcription
hazard. \textbf{Resolved to $1/(4\alpha^2)$} three ways: by derivation
(\S\ref{sec:omega}), by consistency with the paper's own seed $\omega_0=2/\gamma$, and
against \src{[GV]}. Recorded so it need not be re-litigated. & \sev{S1}\newline\emph{if misread}\\[2pt]

\textbf{T-7} & \src{[T]} \S2.2 &
The method is designed and benchmarked with \textbf{Jacobi} (diagonal) preconditioning,

exchanged halo volume. & \sev{S2}\\[2pt]

\section{Resolution of the P-CSI \texorpdfstring{$\omega$}{omega} recurrence (T-6)}
\label{sec:omega}

Let the preconditioned operator have spectrum in $[\nu,\mu]$ and set
$\theta=\tfrac12(\mu+\nu)$, $\delta_{\mathrm{h}}=\tfrac12(\mu-\nu)$,
$\sigma_1=\theta/\delta_{\mathrm{h}}$. The standard three-term form \src{[GV]} is
$\rho_0=1/\sigma_1$, $\rho_k=1/(2\sigma_1-\rho_{k-1})$ and
$\Delta x_k=\rho_k\rho_{k-1}\Delta x_{k-1}+(2\rho_k/\delta_{\mathrm{h}})\Mi r_k$.
With the paper's variables $\alpha=2/(\mu-\nu)=1/\delta_{\mathrm{h}}$ and
$\gamma=\theta$, put $\omega_k:=2\rho_k/\delta_{\mathrm{h}}$. Then
\begin{equation}
  \omega_k=\frac{2}{\delta_{\mathrm{h}}(2\sigma_1-\rho_{k-1})}
         =\frac{2}{2\theta-\delta_{\mathrm{h}}\rho_{k-1}},
  \qquad \rho_{k-1}=\tfrac12\delta_{\mathrm{h}}\omega_{k-1},
\end{equation}
so that
\begin{equation}
  \omega_k=\frac{1}{\theta-\tfrac14\delta_{\mathrm{h}}^{2}\,\omega_{k-1}}
         =\boxed{\ \frac{1}{\gamma-\dfrac{\omega_{k-1}}{4\alpha^{2}}}\ }
\end{equation}
since $\delta_{\mathrm{h}}=1/\alpha$. The reading $\tfrac14\alpha^2$ is therefore
incorrect. Two independent confirmations: the paper's own seed $\omega_0=2/\gamma$
follows from $\rho_0=1/\sigma_1$ only under this reading; and a direct numerical test
shows the derived coefficient meets the Golub--Varga convergence bound at
$k=10,20,40$ while the misread one does not. Note also that the initial step uses
$\Delta x_0=\gamma^{-1}\Mi r_0$, \emph{not} $\omega_0$; a reader assuming otherwise is
off by exactly a factor of two on the first step.
